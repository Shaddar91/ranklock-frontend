Put Warden on your practice list this week, and run the same check on every
other name you find beside his. His pick rate reached 32.5% on the 2026-08-22
patch, up 8.6 points from 23.9% on the 2026-08-12 patch and the largest
pick-rate gain of any hero on the [current patch](/patches/), and his win rate
climbed with it, 1.42 points to 51.6% across 53,279 matches inside that
patch's window. Nothing in the 2026-08-22 changelog names him. Two columns
moving together is what separates a hero who got better from a hero everyone
is trying once, and only the first kind is worth the queue time.

## The four gainers that do not earn the same answer

Leave the other four names on the gainer list where they are, because the
second column never followed them. Mina gained 6.1 points of pick rate for a
0.45-point win-rate move, Paradox gained 5.7 points for 0.03, Abrams gained
1.8 points for a 0.12-point loss, and Rem gained 1.8 points for a 0.01-point
loss. Four heroes got picked more and kept the win rate they already had,
which is the ordinary shape of a gainer list: attention moving, results
standing still. Warden is the only name on it whose win rate moved more than
half a point alongside its pick rate.

## If you take the pick, take the map early

Cash the practice in before the half hour, because his own curve says the
value is front-loaded. He wins 58.0% of matches decided under 30 minutes,
52.6% of those running 30 to 45 and 49.1% of those past 45, a fall of 8.8
points end to end, so every objective you take early is worth more on him than
the same objective twenty minutes later. Play him as the hero who closes
games rather than the one who survives them, and read [how to play
Warden](/heroes/warden/guide/) for the ability order and the buys that go with
that plan.

## Read the changelog before you credit it

Check the notes for the hero's own name first, because on this release they
explain somebody else. The 2026-08-22 changelog carries twelve lines and not
one of them names Warden. Two are hero-agnostic: Radiant Regeneration's heal
on cast dropped from 70 to 65, and Restorative Locket's Spirit Resistance
dropped from 10% to 8%. The other ten belong to Celeste, and they read mostly
as a kit-wide pull-back with one line running the other way: Dazzling Trick's
base cooldown grew from 32s to 34s, its tier-two barrier lost value on the
flat number while gaining scaling, its tier-three cooldown reduction deepened
from -18s to -20s even as its silence shrank from +1.5s to +1.25s, Radiant
Daggers' Spirit Amp per stack dropped from +8% to +7% at base while its
tier-three bonus grew from +3% to +4%, and Shining Wonder lost bounce range,
base damage, and roughly a third of its spirit scaling before getting some of
that scaling back at tier two.

Celeste is the hero those lines describe, and she is worth keeping if you
already play her. Her pick rate fell 4.2 points to 28.4% on the same release
while her win rate rose 0.15 points to 52.6%, which you can confirm on
[Celeste's page](/heroes/celeste/). Players walked away from a hero whose kit
had just been cut in ten places and whose results held up anyway. Nothing in
the same notes explains Warden's rise, and the honest answer is that the two
shapes sit on one release without a documented line connecting them.

Widen the search and the gap gets harder to explain rather than easier.
Warden's name is absent from the 2026-08-12 changelog too, and from the one
before that on 2026-07-28. Three consecutive releases without a single logged
change to the hero, and the pick rate still climbed 8.6 points on the third.
Whatever moved the roster toward him did not come from a line in his own kit.

## What a nerf you can trust looks like

Set that beside a hero the notes did explain, so you know agreement when you
see it. McGinnis took five separate changes on the 2026-08-12 release: bullet
damage base and growth values cut by 5%, Spirit Resist per boon cut from
+0.625% to +0.35%, Mini Turrets losing their decaying resistance window and
their ability to shoot heroes crossing on ziplines, offset only partly by a
turret health increase. Pick rate fell 5.1 points and win rate fell 4.13
points together, both moving the direction the notes predicted, and you can
check them on [McGinnis's page](/heroes/mcginnis/). A hero that got measurably
worse because the changelog said so is the case you act on the same night.
Warden is the case where you wait for the second column to agree, which it
did.

## Two win rates, and the one your queue cares about

Use the patch-window number when the question is this week and the all-window
number when the question is the hero. Warden's patch-window win rate is 51.6%
over 53,279 matches, counted only inside the current release. His all-window
rate on the [heroes grid](/heroes/) and the [tier list](/tier-list/) reads
50.9% over 1,268,192 picks, a far larger sample answering how he has performed
across the whole tracked span. Both are correct, and quoting one where the
other belongs is how a stat gets misused. The 0.7 points between them stays
small because 53,279 matches is still a fraction of 1,268,192, and the gap
will keep narrowing toward the all-window figure as the release ages.

## Running the check yourself next time

Do the same two-column read on the next release and you will not need this
post. RankLock has 123 patches registered, with 2026-08-22 marked current and
2026-08-12 the entry before it, and every patch page carries the same shape:
gainers, losers, and the win-rate delta behind each one, broken out by
bracket, next to a changelog that sometimes explains the move and sometimes
does not. [How to read the patch tracker](/blog/patch-tracker-meta-shifts/)
gives the full reading order.

Some gainers on the next page will trace straight back to a named buff, and
those you can take at face value. Others will move with nothing written about
them, and the answer there is the one this page gave for Warden: check whether
the win rate came along, and when it did, spend the practice hours without
waiting for a reason you can quote.
