Build the gun and refuse the armour, because the souls you spend staying alive
are the souls that never reach Lucky Shot. [Healing
Tempo](/items/1427630806/) wins 54.8% of the 264,015 Venator games it was
bought in and Lucky Shot 54.0% of 1,021,467, while Metal Skin wins 35.1% of
360,507, on a hero who wins 45.3% of his 2,012,180 games in the 2026-05-01 to
2026-09-03 window. Nearly twenty points separate the best qualifying buy from
the worst, the widest spread on any of the four most-picked heroes.

## Pick the ground before you pick the fight

Choose the firing position first, because Venator cannot fix a bad one. His
kit is Consecrating Grenade, Gutshot and Hex-Lined Snap Trap, with Ira Domini
as the fourth ability, and not one of them moves him out of trouble. The
[Venator hero page](/heroes/venator/) reads 7.1 kills, 7.2 deaths and 11.5
assists a game across the window, 42,727 souls of average net worth and an
average match of 37:38. Kills and deaths in balance on a hero that loses 54.7%
of its games says the individual fights are about even and the games are being
lost around them, which makes his value arrive with the mid-game items and
makes the second-lowest average net worth of the four most-picked heroes
matter as much as any ability. Where the trap and the wall stun go is the
[Venator guide](/heroes/venator/guide/).

## The items that beat the baseline

Buy the gun lines in the order the table gives them. Counting items with at
least 146,674 Venator games behind them, 2% of the hero's largest item sample,
the top of the 2026-05-01 to 2026-09-03 table is Healing Tempo at 54.8% over
264,015 games, Lucky Shot at 54.0% over 1,021,467, Frenzy at 53.7% over
427,294, [Spellslinger](/items/2221211450/) at 53.6% over 1,780,148, Silencer
at 53.2% over 2,655,561, Glass Cannon at 52.6% over 1,075,360 and Siphon
Bullets at 52.1% over 1,945,604.

Every one of those beats the hero by between 6.8 and 9.5 points, which is a
larger signal here than the same figure would be on Seven, because Venator's
own rate is 45.3%. Lucky Shot, Frenzy, Glass Cannon and Silencer are the same
late gun slots that lead Haze's table. On her they win six games in ten; on
Venator they win a little over half, which is the measured cost of picking a
45.3% hero and building it well. Healing Tempo tops the list on the smallest
sample of the seven, so hold that one row loosely until the count grows.

## The worst buy on any of these four heroes

Take Metal Skin out of the build entirely. It wins 35.1% of the 360,507
Venator games it appears in, which is to say it loses 64.9% of them, and
across every hero on the [items page](/items/) the same item wins 40.1% of
9,265,427 games. Venator does five points worse with it than the roster does,
and it is the lowest qualifying row on any of the four most-picked heroes.

The rows under it repeat the shape. Slowing Hex wins 40.1% of 181,872 Venator
games, Bullet Resilience 40.5% of 184,810 and Toxic Bullets 41.7% of 541,755.
[Metal Skin](/items/1378931225/) and Bullet Resilience are both armour bought
by a Venator who is already being killed, so the table cannot separate the
item's effect from the state of the game that prompted the buy. The reading
that survives that caveat is the useful one, and the buy list shows how common
the habit is. Battle Vest is the most-bought Venator item at 7,333,694 games
and wins 45.8% of them, with Bullet Lifesteal next at 7,269,965 games and
46.1%.

## The opponent changes your pace, not your build

Expect the same crossbow build against everyone. Venator loses 59.5% of his
454,553 matches against Seven, the worst raw row on the hero, and it is one of
his better results. RankLock's build page predicts every matchup from the two
overall win rates, 50% plus Venator's rate minus the opponent's, over the five
rank brackets summed for the 2026-05-01 to 2026-09-03 window, with a floor of
9,091 matches that 37 opponents clear. Seven wins 55.9% of everything, so the
lane predicts 39.4% and Venator's 40.5% beats it by 1.1 points. The same row
on [Seven's page](/heroes/seven/) is that hero's worst return, and losing six
games in ten can be an above-expectation result when the opponent is the best
hero on the roster.

His largest gap is [Vindicta](/heroes/vindicta/), 47.1% against a predicted
45.8% over 240,725 matches, 1.3 points. Wraith and Celeste sit within six
hundredths of a point of the Seven row, so read Seven as one positive row
among several rather than the second best. The negative side is the
shallowest of the four most-picked heroes: Pocket at 47.6% against a predicted
48.1% over 203,841 matches, and The Doorman at 44.6% against 45.0% over
210,107. Neither reaches half a point.

Almost no matchup structure survives that, so the build does not change with
the opponent on this hero. The pace does. Into Vindicta and Seven the numbers
say the lane returns more than expected, so the gun items can come a little
earlier. Into Pocket and The Doorman the lane is exactly as hard as the
ratings say, and the pick decision was made at the draft screen.

## Your band moves the number

Read your own band's row before you take him, because most players sit near
the bottom of his curve. On the [heroes grid](/heroes/) filtered by band,
Venator wins 46.5% at Initiate, then drops to 44.8% at Seeker and 44.3% at
Alchemist, his worst band. From there it climbs: 44.5% at Arcanist and
Ritualist, 45.1% at Emissary, 44.9% at Archon, 45.5% at Oracle, 46.0% at
Phantom, 46.2% at Ascendant and 46.7% at Eternus, all in the 2026-05-01 to
2026-09-03 window.

That is 2.4 points recovered between Alchemist and Eternus, and every band is
still under the 47.0% cut that separates D from F. Near the bottom of the
curve, treat the hero's rate as a cost you are choosing to pay, and pay it
only for a hero you play far better than the field. Near the top you get
46.7%, still an F number, and 2.4 points better than the Alchemist row.

## Where the two published builds part

Two published Venator builds reach RankLock's list, the fewest of the four
heroes, and they split on one late slot. Thirty-one weekly favorites separate
them: 12,272 for the build by Account 1183614423, version 34, and 12,241 for
the build by Account 393148161, version 31. Both names carry the same
streaming handle, so both are cited by author and favorites, and the shared
handle also means the two cannot be read as independent opinions.

Both are gun builds and both open on Active Reload, Battle Vest, Kinetic Dash
and Enduring Speed. The 12,272 build adds Extra Stamina, Weighted Shots,
Recharging Rush and Sprint Boots to that opening; the 12,241 build adds
Headshot Booster, Cultist Sacrifice, Slowing Hex and Superior Cooldown. The
measured rates on the shared four are 42.8% for Active Reload over 427,058
games, 45.8% for Battle Vest, 45.2% for Kinetic Dash over 816,141 and 47.0%
for Enduring Speed over 5,114,665, all laning items and all near the hero's
baseline, as laning items are.

They part at the end, and the data takes a side. The 12,241 build closes on
Juggernaut, Crippling Headshot, Glass Cannon, Ricochet and Capacitor. The
12,272 build's end block is Juggernaut, Frenzy and Ricochet, and it buys
Crippling Headshot and Glass Cannon a tier earlier, in a mid-game and
late-game block alongside Toxic Bullets, Bullet Lifesteal, Vampiric Burst,
Lucky Shot, Inhibitor and Silencer. Juggernaut, at 47.9% over 1,479,165 games,
and Ricochet, at 50.8% over 952,444, are the two items both close on. The
split that matters is Frenzy against Capacitor: Frenzy wins 53.7% of 427,294
Venator games and sits on the item list two sections up, while Capacitor has
49,749 Venator games behind it and does not clear the sample floor. On that
one slot the data sides with the 12,272 build. The [Venator build
page](/heroes/venator/build/) has both builds next to the measured sets and
the buy order, with the rank filter on the item table for players who want
their own band's numbers.
