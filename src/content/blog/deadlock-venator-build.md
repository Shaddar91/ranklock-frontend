Venator was picked 2,012,180 times in the 2026-05-01 to 2026-09-03 window,
fourth on the roster, and won 45.3% of those games, second lowest of the 38
heroes. Only Sinclair, at 44.7% on 802,794 picks, wins less often, and
Sinclair is picked less than half as much. Bebop, the only other hero over two
million picks below the 47.0% F cut, wins 1.2 points more often.

## Two million picks, 45.3%

The [Venator hero page](/heroes/venator/) reads 7.1 kills, 7.2 deaths and 11.5
assists a game across the window, 42,727 souls of average net worth and an
average match of 37:38. Kills and deaths in balance on a hero that loses 54.7%
of its games says the individual fights are about even and the games are being
lost around them. Venator's kit is Consecrating Grenade, Gutshot and Hex-Lined
Snap Trap, with Ira Domini as the fourth ability, and both published builds
treat the hero as a gun carry: the item lines that win are gun lines, and the
buys that lose are the defensive ones. What a team gets from the pick is a
ranged damage dealer whose value arrives with the mid-game items, which is why
the average net worth, the second lowest of the four most-picked heroes,
matters as much as any ability.

## The items that beat the baseline

Because Venator's own rate is 45.3%, an item at 53% is a larger signal here
than the same figure would be on Seven. Counting items with at least 146,674
Venator games behind them, 2% of the hero's largest item sample, the top of
the 2026-05-01 to 2026-09-03 table is [Healing Tempo](/items/1427630806/) at
54.8% over 264,015 games, Lucky Shot at 54.0% over 1,021,467, Frenzy at 53.7%
over 427,294, [Spellslinger](/items/2221211450/) at 53.6% over 1,780,148,
Silencer at 53.2% over 2,655,561, Glass Cannon at 52.6% over 1,075,360 and
Siphon Bullets at 52.1% over 1,945,604.

Every one of those beats the hero by between 6.8 and 9.5 points, and Lucky
Shot, Frenzy, Glass Cannon and Silencer are the late gun slots that also lead
Haze's table. The difference is where the line sits. On Haze those four items
win six games in ten; on Venator they win a little over half, which is the
measured cost of picking a 45.3% hero and building it well. Healing Tempo at
the top on the smallest sample of the seven is the one line to hold loosely
until the count grows.

## The worst item line in the batch

Metal Skin wins 35.1% of the 360,507 Venator games it appears in, which means
it loses 64.9% of them. On the [items page](/items/) the same item wins 40.1%
of 9,265,427 games across every hero, so Venator does five points worse with
it than the roster does, and it is the lowest qualifying row on any of the
four most-picked heroes. [Metal Skin](/items/1378931225/) is the single
clearest stop in any of the four tables.

The rows under it follow the same shape. Slowing Hex wins 40.1% of 181,872
Venator games, Bullet Resilience 40.5% of 184,810 and Toxic Bullets 41.7% of
541,755. Metal Skin and Bullet Resilience are both armour bought by a Venator
who is already being killed, and the table cannot separate the item's effect
from the state of the game that prompted it. The reading that survives that
caveat is still useful: a Venator spending on armour is a Venator whose game
is already going badly, and the souls are better spent reaching Lucky Shot.
That is the common mistake on this hero, and it shows up in the most-bought
list too. Battle Vest is the most-bought Venator item at 7,333,694 games and
wins 45.8% of them, and Bullet Lifesteal follows at 7,269,965 games and 46.1%.

## The Seven row, read from the other side

Venator loses 59.5% of its 454,553 matches against Seven, the worst raw row
on the hero, and it is one of Venator's better results. RankLock's build page
predicts every matchup from the two overall win rates, 50% plus Venator's rate
minus the opponent's, over the five rank brackets summed for the 2026-05-01 to
2026-09-03 window, with a floor of 9,091 matches that 37 opponents clear.
Seven wins 55.9% of everything, so the prediction for the lane is 39.4%, and
Venator's 40.5% beats it by 1.1 points. The same row on [Seven's
page](/heroes/seven/) is that hero's worst return. Losing six games in ten can
be an above-expectation result when the opponent is the best hero on the
roster.

The largest gap on the hero is [Vindicta](/heroes/vindicta/), 47.1% against a
predicted 45.8% over 240,725 matches, 1.3 points. Wraith and Celeste sit
within six hundredths of a point of the Seven row, so Seven is one of the
positive rows rather than the second best. The negative side is the shallowest
of the four most-picked heroes: Pocket, 47.6% against a predicted 48.1% over
203,841 matches, and The Doorman, 44.6% against 45.0% over 210,107. Neither
reaches half a point.

Venator has almost no matchup structure. It loses to most of the roster at the rate its own rating
predicts, and the two lanes with the least room are still within half a point
of prediction. The build does not change with the opponent on this hero; the
pace does. Into Vindicta and Seven the numbers say the lane returns more than
expected, so the gun items can come a little earlier. Into Pocket and The
Doorman the lane is exactly as hard as the ratings say, and the pick decision
was made at the draft screen.

## The climb helps

On the [heroes grid](/heroes/) filtered by band, Venator wins 46.5% at
Initiate, then drops to 44.8% at Seeker and 44.3% at Alchemist, its worst
band. From there it climbs: 44.5% at Arcanist and Ritualist, 45.1% at
Emissary, 44.9% at Archon, 45.5% at Oracle, 46.0% at Phantom, 46.2% at
Ascendant and 46.7% at Eternus, all in the 2026-05-01 to 2026-09-03 window.
That is 2.4 points recovered between Alchemist and Eternus, and every band is
still under the 47.0% cut that separates D from F.

A player near the bottom of that curve should read the hero's rate as a cost
they are choosing to pay, and pay it only for a hero they play far better than
the field. A player near the top gets 46.7%, still an F number, but 2.4 points
better than the Alchemist row.

## Two published builds, and where they part

Two published Venator builds reach RankLock's list, the fewest of the four
heroes, and 31 weekly favorites separate them: 12,272 for the build by Account
1183614423, version 34, and 12,241 for the build by Account 393148161, version
31. Both names carry the same streaming handle, so both are cited by author and
favorites, and the shared handle also means the two cannot be read as
independent opinions.

Both are gun builds and both open on Active Reload, Battle Vest, Kinetic Dash
and Enduring Speed. The 12,272 build adds Extra Stamina, Weighted Shots,
Recharging Rush and Sprint Boots to that opening; the 12,241 build adds
Headshot Booster, Cultist Sacrifice, Slowing Hex and Superior Cooldown. The
measured rates on the shared four are 42.8% for Active Reload over 427,058
games, 45.8% for Battle Vest, 45.2% for Kinetic Dash over 816,141 and 47.0%
for Enduring Speed over 5,114,665, all laning items and all near the hero's
baseline, as laning items are.

They part at the end. The 12,241 build closes on Juggernaut, Crippling
Headshot, Glass Cannon, Ricochet and Capacitor. The 12,272 build's end block is
Juggernaut, Frenzy and Ricochet, and it buys Crippling Headshot and Glass
Cannon a tier earlier, in a mid-game and late-game block alongside Toxic
Bullets, Bullet Lifesteal, Vampiric Burst, Lucky Shot, Inhibitor and Silencer.
Juggernaut, at 47.9% over 1,479,165 games, and Ricochet, at 50.8% over
952,444, are the two items both close on. The split that matters is Frenzy
against Capacitor: Frenzy wins 53.7% of 427,294 Venator games and sits on the
item list two sections up, while Capacitor has 49,749 Venator games behind it
and does not clear the sample floor. On that one slot the data sides with the
12,272 build. The [Venator build page](/heroes/venator/build/) has both builds
next to the measured sets and the buy order, with the rank filter on the item
table for players who want their own band's numbers.
