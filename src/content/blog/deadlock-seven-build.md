Buy more of the area you already fill, and stop paying for armour. [Spirit
Burn](/items/343572757/) leads Seven's item table at 61.5% over 1,333,235
games while Metal Skin wins 44.4% of the 415,723 Seven games it appears in, on
a hero who wins 55.9% of his 2,053,279 games in the 2026-05-01 to 2026-09-03
window, the highest rate of the 38 heroes on the roster. Seventeen points
between the best and the worst qualifying buy is most of what a Seven player
still has to get right.

## What the shopping is actually buying

Every good slot on this hero buys the same thing twice. Lightning Ball travels
down a line and slows what it damages, Static Charge stuns and damages the
ground under whoever it is stuck to, Power Surge puts shock damage on the
bullets that bounces off the body they hit, and Storm Cloud expands around
Seven and needs line of sight to reach anything. All four scale with spirit,
radius and duration, which is why his table is a list of those three rather
than a list of gun items. His line on the [Seven hero page](/heroes/seven/)
reads 6.3 kills, 6.9 deaths and 13.1 assists a game across the window, 45,804
souls of average net worth and an average match of 37:41. Fewer kills than
Haze, more assists, more souls: he gets paid for being near fights rather than
for finishing them. Where to stand while you cast is the [Seven
guide](/heroes/seven/guide/).

## The default build is already a winning build

Leave the everyday buys alone. Seven's most-bought items are already winning
items, which is the one thing that separates him from the other three heroes
in this group. Spirit Lifesteal is bought in 7,691,108 Seven games and
wins 55.7% of them. Mystic Vulnerability, 7,631,890 games, wins 55.7%.
Escalating Exposure, 7,554,980 games, wins 57.1%. Monster Rounds, 6,008,669
games, wins 54.5%, and Superior Duration, 5,732,647 games, wins 56.4%. On Haze
and Bebop the everyday buys sit at the baseline and the winning items are the
ones most players skip; on Seven every one of those five sits within a point
and a half of a 55.9% baseline, so a player who buys nothing clever is already
winning.

## Where the extra souls go

Reach for the lines that make the area bigger and keep it there longer.
Counting items with at least 153,822 Seven games behind them, 2% of the hero's
largest item sample, the top of the 2026-05-01 to 2026-09-03 table is Spirit
Burn at 61.5% over 1,333,235 games, then Diviner's Kevlar at 60.2% over
208,719, [Lightning Scroll](/items/493591231/) at 59.6% over 1,436,600,
Indomitable at 59.3% over 2,004,316, Mercurial Magnum at 58.4% over 992,546,
Greater Expansion at 58.3% over 3,116,091 and Spiritual Overflow at 58.2% over
4,105,658.

The best line beats the hero's own rate by 5.6 points, the narrowest gap in
this batch, because the baseline is already high. A Seven who reaches Spirit
Burn is a Seven whose game ran long enough to afford it, so part of that 61.5%
is the lead that bought the item rather than the item itself. That caveat
applies to every late buy on every hero and bites least here, with a floor of
55.9% before anything late goes in.

## Delete Metal Skin

Take the armour out of the build. Metal Skin wins 44.4% of the 415,723 Seven
games it appears in, 11.5 points under the hero's rate and nearly three points
below the next row, Bullet Resilience at 47.2% over 286,574. Buying armour to
survive a dive is the common Seven mistake, and the table is blunt about it.
The duration and spirit lines pay for themselves and the armour lines never
do.

## The lane that looks free and is not

Do not read a 59.5% row as a free lane. Seven wins 59.5% of his 454,553
matches against Venator in the window, one of his highest raw rows, and it is
his worst result once the two ratings are priced in. RankLock's build page
predicts each matchup from the overall win rates, 50% plus Seven's rate minus
the opponent's, and reports the gap. Venator wins 45.3% of its games, so the
lane predicts 60.6% and 59.5% falls 1.1 points short of it. A high row against
a weak hero is the ordinary outcome of being a strong hero.

Sinclair is the other row shaped that way, 60.2% over 149,018 matches where
61.2% was predicted. Infernus lands on the same rounded gap two hundredths of
a point away, so treat Venator and Sinclair as a pair rather than a ranking.
The positive rows are Mo & Krill at 56.1% against a predicted 54.8% over
205,055 matches and [Graves](/heroes/graves/) at 51.9% against 50.7% over
380,830. All four gaps are under 1.3 points across the five brackets summed,
with a floor of 9,736 matches that 37 opponents clear, so none of them is a
counter-pick.

What changes is the pace. Into Graves and Mo & Krill the lane returns more
than the ratings say, so the early Extra Spirit into Duration Extender path
pays sooner. Into [Venator](/heroes/venator/) expect to win six games in ten
and play the lane like it can still be lost. The [matchups
guide](/blog/hero-matchups-101/) covers reading a row's count before its rate.

## Practice on him keeps its value

Take the all-ranks number at face value on this hero, which is rare enough on
this site to be worth stating. Filtered by band on the [heroes
grid](/heroes/), Seven wins 55.7% at Initiate, 55.2% at Seeker, 55.8% at
Alchemist, 55.9% at Arcanist, 56.3% at Ritualist, 56.2% at Emissary, 56.2% at
Archon, 56.3% at Oracle, 56.1% at Phantom, 55.6% at Ascendant and 56.0% at
Eternus, all in the 2026-05-01 to 2026-09-03 window. The whole range is 1.1
points and every band clears the 52.5% S cut.

Two things follow for a player. The 55.9% on the hero page describes your own
lobbies whatever band you sit in, and the hours you put into him keep paying
as you climb, because nothing in the data says his game plan gets answered
more often in stronger lobbies. Haze loses 4.5 points between Alchemist and
Eternus; Seven loses nothing.

## The one slot the published builds leave open

Three published Seven builds reach RankLock's list, and they disagree about a
single late item. The most favorited, at 10,705 weekly favorites by Account
371328371, carries a name with profanity in it, and the third, at 8,247 by
Account 1183614423, carries a streaming handle, so both are cited here by
author and favorites. The second, "Кошмар электрика Виталия" by Account
457310025 at 8,260, is printed as published.

Lane on the top build and you buy Monster Rounds, Opening Rounds, Extra
Spirit, Enchanter's Emblem, Spirit Shredder Bullets, Intensifying Magazine,
Surge of Power and Enduring Speed. Its post-lane block is Duration Extender,
Spirit Lifesteal, Toxic Bullets, Mystic Vulnerability, Escalating Exposure,
Superior Duration and Spiritual Overflow, which holds four of the five
most-bought items from above and measures between 55.0% for Toxic Bullets over
3,635,112 games and 58.2% for Spiritual Overflow. Its late block is Infuser,
Boundless Spirit, Unstoppable, Greater Expansion, Lightning Scroll, Spirit
Burn and Transcendent Cooldown, which is where the top of the win-rate table
lives: Boundless Spirit at 58.0% over 3,832,936 games, Greater Expansion at
58.3%, Lightning Scroll at 59.6% and Spirit Burn at 61.5%.

The second build reaches the same place by a shorter road. It lanes on Extra
Spirit, Monster Rounds, Enchanter's Emblem, Improved Spirit, Suppressor and
Enduring Speed, goes Active Reload, Surge of Power, Duration Extender, Spirit
Lifesteal and Fleetfoot after lane, then Superior Duration, Boundless Spirit
and Toxic Bullets, and closes on Infuser, Unstoppable, Escalating Exposure,
Spiritual Overflow, Inhibitor, Spirit Burn and Lightning Scroll. Indomitable,
59.3% over two million games, is in neither core, and the top build keeps it
in a situational list. The [Seven build page](/heroes/seven/build/) sets all
three beside the measured item sets and the buy order, and on this hero the
question worth taking there is which late slot Indomitable earns.
