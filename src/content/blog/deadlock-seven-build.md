Seven wins 55.9% of its 2,053,279 games in the 2026-05-01 to 2026-09-03
window, the highest win rate of the 38 heroes on the roster, and it is also
the third most-picked hero in the data. Those two facts rarely sit together. A
hero that wins this often on a pick base this large is winning in ordinary
lobbies, played by ordinary players, and that changes how much a build guide
can add.

## 55.9% across two million games

The [Seven hero page](/heroes/seven/) reads 6.3 kills, 6.9 deaths and 13.1
assists per game across the window, 45,804 souls of average net worth, the
highest of the four most-picked heroes, and an average match of 37:41. Fewer
kills than Haze, more assists, more souls: Seven gets paid for being near
fights rather than for finishing them. Lightning Ball and Static Charge
control space, Power Surge adds spirit damage to the gun for a window, and
Storm Cloud is a damage field around Seven that touches whole fights. What a
team gets from the pick is sustained area damage that scales with spirit and
duration, and the item table below is a list of ways to buy more of both.

## The item table

Counting items with at least 153,822 Seven games behind them, 2% of the hero's
largest item sample, the top of the table in the 2026-05-01 to 2026-09-03
window is [Spirit Burn](/items/343572757/) at 61.5% over 1,333,235 games, then
Diviner's Kevlar at 60.2% over 208,719, [Lightning
Scroll](/items/493591231/) at 59.6% over 1,436,600, Indomitable at 59.3% over
2,004,316, Mercurial Magnum at 58.4% over 992,546, Greater Expansion at 58.3%
over 3,116,091 and Spiritual Overflow at 58.2% over 4,105,658.

The gap between the best line and the hero's own rate is 5.6 points, narrower
than on any other hero in this batch, because the baseline is already high. A
Seven that reaches Spirit Burn is a Seven whose game went long enough to
afford it, so part of that 61.5% is the lead that paid for the item rather
than the item itself. That caveat applies to every late buy on every hero; it
bites less here, since the floor is 55.9% before any late item goes in.

## What the most-bought items say

Seven's most-bought items are already winning items, which is the one sentence
that separates this hero from the other three. Spirit Lifesteal is bought in
7,691,108 Seven games and wins 55.7% of them. Mystic Vulnerability, 7,631,890
games, wins 55.7%. Escalating Exposure, 7,554,980 games, wins 57.1%. Monster
Rounds, 6,008,669 games, wins 54.5%, and Superior Duration, 5,732,647 games,
wins 56.4%. On Haze and Bebop the everyday buys sit at the hero's baseline and
the winning items are the ones most players skip. On Seven the everyday buys
sit within a point and a half of the baseline on either side, and the baseline
is 55.9%, so the default build is a winning build before anyone thinks about
it.

That leaves less for a guide to fix, and one thing it can. Metal Skin wins
44.4% of the 415,723 Seven games it appears in, 11.5 points under the hero's
rate and nearly three points below the next row in the table. Bullet
Resilience is that next row at 47.2% over 286,574. The common Seven mistake is
buying armour to survive a dive; the table says the duration and spirit lines
pay and the armour lines do not.

## Beating Venator without beating expectation

Seven wins 59.5% of its 454,553 matches against Venator in the window, one of
its highest raw rows on the table, and it is the hero's worst result. Both
statements are true at once, and the reason is the method. RankLock's build
page predicts each matchup from the two overall win rates, 50% plus Seven's
rate minus the opponent's, and reports the gap. Venator wins 45.3% of its
games, so the prediction for the lane is 60.6%, and Seven's 59.5% falls 1.1
points short of it. A high row against a weak hero is the ordinary outcome of
being a strong hero; it only counts as an achievement if it beats what the two
ratings already promised.

Sinclair is the other row shaped like that: Seven wins 60.2% of 149,018
matches where 61.2% was predicted, 1.0 points short. Infernus lands on the
same rounded gap two hundredths of a point away, so Venator and Sinclair are a
pair here rather than a ranking. On the positive side the rows are Mo & Krill,
56.1% against a predicted 54.8% over 205,055 matches, and
[Graves](/heroes/graves/), 51.9% against 50.7% over 380,830. All four gaps are
under 1.3 points across the five brackets summed, with a floor of 9,736
matches that 37 opponents clear, so none of them is a counter-pick.

The build changes at the edges. Into Graves and Mo & Krill the lane returns
more than the ratings say, so the early Extra Spirit into Duration Extender
path pays sooner. Into [Venator](/heroes/venator/) the row says a Seven player
should expect to win six games in ten and should not read the lane as free.
The [matchups guide](/blog/hero-matchups-101/) covers reading a row's count
before its rate.

## The flattest curve in the top four

Filter the [heroes grid](/heroes/) by band and Seven barely moves. It wins
55.7% at Initiate, 55.2% at Seeker, 55.8% at Alchemist, 55.9% at Arcanist,
56.3% at Ritualist, 56.2% at Emissary, 56.2% at Archon, 56.3% at Oracle, 56.1%
at Phantom, 55.6% at Ascendant and 56.0% at Eternus, all in the 2026-05-01 to
2026-09-03 window. The whole range is 1.1 points, and every band clears the
52.5% S cut.

Two things follow for a player. The all-ranks 55.9% on the hero page describes
your own lobbies whatever band you sit in, which is rare enough on this site
to be worth stating. And practice on Seven keeps paying as you climb, because
nothing in the data says the hero's game plan gets answered more often in
stronger lobbies. Haze loses 4.5 points between Alchemist and Eternus; Seven
loses nothing.

## Three published builds, phase by phase

Three published Seven builds reach RankLock's list. The most favorited, at
10,705 weekly favorites by Account 371328371, carries a name with profanity in
it, and the third, at 8,247 by Account 1183614423, carries a streaming handle,
so both are cited here by author and favorites. The second, "Кошмар электрика
Виталия" by Account 457310025 at 8,260, is printed as published.

The top build's laning block is Monster Rounds, Opening Rounds, Extra Spirit,
Enchanter's Emblem, Spirit Shredder Bullets, Intensifying Magazine, Surge of
Power and Enduring Speed. Its post-lane block is Duration Extender, Spirit
Lifesteal, Toxic Bullets, Mystic Vulnerability, Escalating Exposure, Superior
Duration and Spiritual Overflow, which holds four of the five most-bought items
from above, measured between 55.0% for Toxic Bullets over 3,635,112 games and
58.2% for Spiritual Overflow. Its late block is Infuser, Boundless Spirit,
Unstoppable, Greater Expansion, Lightning Scroll, Spirit Burn and Transcendent
Cooldown, and that is where the top of the win-rate table lives: Boundless
Spirit at 58.0% over 3,832,936 games, Greater Expansion at 58.3%, Lightning
Scroll at 59.6%, Spirit Burn at 61.5%.

The second build reaches the same place by a shorter road. It lanes on Extra
Spirit, Monster Rounds, Enchanter's Emblem, Improved Spirit, Suppressor and
Enduring Speed, goes Active Reload, Surge of Power, Duration Extender, Spirit
Lifesteal and Fleetfoot after lane, then Superior Duration, Boundless Spirit
and Toxic Bullets, and closes on Infuser, Unstoppable, Escalating Exposure,
Spiritual Overflow, Inhibitor, Spirit Burn and Lightning Scroll. Indomitable,
59.3% over two million games, is in neither core; the top build keeps it in a
situational list. The [Seven build page](/heroes/seven/build/) sets all three
builds beside the measured item sets and the buy order, and on this hero the
open question is which late slot Indomitable earns.
