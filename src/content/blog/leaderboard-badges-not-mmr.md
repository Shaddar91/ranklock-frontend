Open the [leaderboard](/leaderboard) and the first thing to notice is what
orders it. Players sort by badge tier, the emblem the ranked ladder grants, and
at the time of writing the entire top 50 holds the same one: Eternus VI, the
highest badge in the game. Once every player on the page shares an emblem, the
emblem can no longer rank them. Something else has to.

## How the ladder is ordered

RankLock's leaderboard follows the ranked ladder itself. Twelve named tiers run
from Obscurus through Initiate, Seeker, Alchemist, Arcanist, Ritualist,
Emissary, Archon, Oracle, Phantom, and Ascendant up to Eternus, each split into
subranks I through VI. The top of the ladder, Eternus VI, is badge 116, and the
[heroes grid](/heroes) exposes the same ordering through its rank-band filter.

A badge is the game's own public judgment of your ranked play, and the subranks
make it granular: a climb through one tier is six measured steps, so a player
at Archon IV and a player at Archon II wear the same tier name but sit two
steps apart. The board orders players by badge and subrank together.

Deep in the ladder that is enough, because players are spread thin across
tiers and subranks. At the ceiling the spreading stops: everyone who has maxed
the emblem system wears the same badge, and the board's ordering question lands
on their records.

## What separates players at the ceiling

With the top 50 pinned at Eternus VI, the leaderboard falls back on
performance: win-rate and match volume. At the time of writing, the #1 player,
"smoke", holds a 53.4% win-rate over 712 matches, while the #3 player,
"GenericEdBoi", holds 49.5% over 735 matches. Same emblem, different records,
and the records are what separate them on the page.

For anyone climbing, the snapshot reframes what "reaching #1" means. Until you
hit the ceiling, the distance between you and the top of the board is badge
distance, measured in tiers and subranks. Past the ceiling, the distance turns
into record distance: win percentage points over hundreds of matches, where a
single point costs weeks of consistent play.

The two records on the board right now make that concrete. The gap between
"smoke" at 53.4% and "GenericEdBoi" at 49.5% is about four points of win-rate,
built over samples of more than 700 matches each, and both players hold the
same Eternus VI badge. At that altitude the emblem system has nothing left to
award, so the argument about who is best happens entirely in the record
columns.

That also holds a lesson for the rest of the ladder. A badge says where you
sit. A win-rate over a counted sample says what you did there. The first is the
game's judgment; the second is yours, measured.

## Why there is no MMR

Players arriving from other games go looking for a matchmaking rating, one
hidden number that says exactly how good you are. RankLock does not publish
one, anywhere. The [items page](/items) labels its own stats "by badge tier,
not MMR", and the leaderboard follows the same rule. The site reports the
emblem system the game exposes and stops there.

The absence is deliberate. A published MMR would be a number the site cannot
trace to match records, and invented precision is worse than an admitted gap.
What you get instead is a usable benchmark: your badge tells you the band, and
the band-filtered stats across the site tell you how players ranked like you
actually perform. [What RankLock computes and what it does
not](/blog/computed-vs-raw-match-data) covers that line in full.

Badges have their own blind spot, and it is worth naming. A badge is coarse:
everyone inside one subrank wears the same emblem even though their recent form
differs, and a badge moves slowly enough that it describes where you were as
much as where you are. That is why the site brackets its stats by band rather
than by individual emblem, because the band is the comparison group that stays
meaningful between promotions.

None of this makes the leaderboard less worth reading. It makes it honest about
what it measures: a public ladder ordered by the emblems the game granted and
the records the matches produced. If you want a single secret number that
settles every argument, you will not find it here, and you should be suspicious
of anywhere that claims to have one.

## Reading your own climb

- **Benchmark at your badge.** The [heroes grid](/heroes) filters stats by rank
  band and the [items page](/items) brackets its numbers from Initiate through
  Eternus, so compare yourself against players the ladder ranks like you, not
  against the top of the board.
- **Let the top 50 be its own contest.** Below the ceiling, your climb is the
  next subrank. At Eternus VI, the contest turns into win-rate and volume,
  because nothing else is left to separate the players there.
- **Read rate and volume together.** A 53.4% win-rate over 712 matches and a
  49.5% over 735 are both Eternus VI records; once badges run out, the board
  treats the record as the ranking signal.
- **Track your pace alongside your badge.** The [Lane Lab
  curves](/blog/lane-lab-first-ten-minutes) let you compare your economy
  against the median at your rank and the rank above you, which is the
  measurable part of a climb between emblems.

The leaderboard's real message is that emblems run out. When they do, only the
record keeps ranking you.
