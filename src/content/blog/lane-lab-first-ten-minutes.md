Players argue about the laning phase from feel. [Lane Lab](/lane-lab) measures
it instead: a median souls curve built from millions of matches, a farm curve
beside it, and a table that attaches a win probability to each 9-minute souls
threshold. This guide walks through what the page shows and how to use it
before your next session. All figures are as of the 2026-08-22 patch.

## The median curve

[Lane Lab](/lane-lab) tracks cumulative souls over the course of a game. The
median player holds about 6,060 souls at 9:00, about 12,000 at 15:00, and about
30,800 at 30:00. The farm curve runs beside it: 6.7 last-hits at 3:00, 14.0 at
6:00, and 20.8 at 9:00.

Between minutes three and nine the median player adds about 14 last-hits. If
your own six-minute mark sits well under 14.0, the gap on your souls curve at
9:00 is already written. The page also lets you read the curve for any rank
against any other rank, so you can compare the pace your band plays at with the
band you are climbing toward.

One more anchor for the late game: the median roughly quintuples between the
9:00 and 30:00 marks, from about 6,060 souls to about 30,800. The early minutes
are where the slope of that line gets decided.

## What 9-minute souls are worth

Across roughly 20 million games, [Lane Lab](/lane-lab) attaches a win
probability to each 9-minute souls threshold:

- **5,000 souls or more** — 47.0% wins. This is the baseline row, the floor the
  other thresholds compare against.
- **6,000 or more** — 51.8%.
- **8,000 or more** — 60.1%.
- **10,000 or more** — 69.5%.
- **14,000 or more** — 87.2%.

Read the jump from the baseline to 8,000 as the headline. Clearing 8,000 souls
by 9:00 moves the measured win probability from 47.0% to 60.1%, a thirteen
point swing bought entirely in the first nine minutes. The middle rows tell the
same story at smaller stakes: holding 6,000 instead of 5,000 is worth about
five points of win probability, and reaching 10,000 prices a dominant laning
phase at 69.5%.

The sample behind the table is the other reason to trust it. Twenty million
games is large enough that these percentages are measurements of the game as it
is played, not streaks or anecdotes.

One caution on the reading: these are associations measured across a population
of games, not promises. A player at 8,500 souls can still throw the match, and
a player at 5,500 can still carry it. The thresholds tell you what the early
economy was worth across millions of recorded games. They cannot play yours.

## Reading the curve while you play

The three farm marks make useful in-game checkpoints. At 3:00, compare your
last-hits against 6.7; missing it early means the wave management is leaking
before any fight has happened. At 6:00 the median sits at 14.0, and at 9:00 at
20.8. You do not need to watch the clock constantly; checking at those three
marks tells you which side of the median you are on while there is still time
to correct it.

The souls side works the same way. At 9:00, stack your total against 6,060.
Above it, you have done your share of the laning phase's work. Well above it,
past 8,000, the historical record says your team wins these games more than six
times in ten.

The mid-game marks work as checkpoints too. At 15:00 the median sits at about
12,000 souls; at 30:00, at about 30,800. If you were ahead of the median at
9:00 and behind it at 15:00, something between the two marks cost you the lead,
and the match scoreboard is where to look for what it was.

These checkpoints are deliberately few. Three farm marks and three souls marks
cover the early and mid game, and each one is a direct comparison against a
measured population. You do not need a spreadsheet to run this loop; you need
six numbers and the honesty to check them after a loss.

## Where early souls come from

The farm curve says the median laner reaches 20.8 last-hits by 9:00, and
last-hits are the income stream you control most directly. The habits behind
that number, wave management, denies, and camp timing, are covered in our
[souls economy basics](/blog/souls-economy-basics) guide, so we will not repeat
them here.

Item timings give the economy a second clock. The average [Improved
Spirit](/items) purchase happens at about 14:00, 843 seconds into the game,
across 7.9 million recorded matches. Set that next to the 15:00 median of
12,000 souls and the picture is consistent: by the quarter-hour mark the median
player has both the souls and the first big purchase that converts them into
fighting power. If you are well behind the curve at that mark, you are also
late to the items your opponent buys on schedule, and the fight you take at
minute fifteen is priced accordingly.

## Using Lane Lab before your next session

- **Find your 9-minute number.** After your next match, compare your souls at
  9:00 against the 6,060 median. Below that line, your laning phase is the leak
  to fix first.
- **Treat 8,000 as the threshold that matters.** The measured win probability
  passes 60% there. If you finish most games under it, the fix starts at minute
  one: the median player already holds 6.7 last-hits at 3:00.
- **Compare against your own band.** Read the curve at your rank against the
  rank above you. The gap between those two lines is the pace difference you
  have to close to climb.

The first ten minutes of a match are measurable now. Measure yours, then raise
it.
