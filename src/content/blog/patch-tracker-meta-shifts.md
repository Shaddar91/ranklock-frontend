RankLock's [patch tracker](/patches/) has logged 123 patches. Each one turns a
few of last month's certainties into guesses, and the tracker is how you catch
up without reading forums for three hours. This guide gives you a reading order
for patch week, plus two habits that keep you from trusting numbers that have
already gone stale.

## What a patch page contains

Every entry on the [patch list](/patches/) opens a page for that patch. The page
shows which heroes gained pick rate after the patch landed, which ones lost it,
and how win-rates moved, broken out by bracket.

A delta is a before-and-after measurement. A pick-rate delta compares how often
a hero gets picked since the patch against the period before it; a win-rate
delta does the same for the share of games won. Both are computed per bracket,
so a single patch can produce several different answers depending on which
ranks you read.

The raw material behind every delta is the same match-record layer the rest of
the site is built on, so a patch page is a computed view over counted games,
not an editorial opinion. [What RankLock computes and what it does
not](/blog/computed-vs-raw-match-data/) explains that pipeline.

The per-bracket split matters for the same reason it matters on the heroes
grid. A buff can lift a hero at low rank and leave high rank untouched, and the
all-ranks column averages those two outcomes into a number that describes
neither. When a hero you play shows up on a patch page, your bracket's row is
the one that concerns you. The bracket rows also tell you when a patch does not
matter to you: if every delta sits outside your pool and outside the heroes you
commonly face, you can bank the quiet week and keep practicing.

## A reading order for patch day

- **Start with your own heroes.** Open the newest patch page and find your main
  picks in the gainer and loser lists. A hero you play showing up on either
  list is the signal to look closer; a patch that leaves your pool untouched
  costs you five minutes and buys you certainty.
- **Use the loser list even when your pool is safe.** A hero you do not play
  can still change your games if you face it often. When a common opponent
  loses win-rate at your bracket, your own picks gain by subtraction.
- **Read the delta at your bracket.** A win-rate move measured at your band
  tells you what your lobbies will feel like next week. The all-brackets move
  tells you what the average lobby feels, and you do not play there.
- **Cross-check on the hero page.** The per-bracket win-rates on each hero page
  update with the data window, so a [page like Lash's](/heroes/31/) confirms
  whether a patch-page delta held once games accumulated.
- **Check your items too.** The [items page](/items/) tracks win-rate and
  average buy timing per item, and a cost or scaling tweak moves those numbers
  before anyone writes a word about it.
- **Give the list a few days.** Early deltas mix the patch's real effect with
  thousands of players experimenting at once. A move that survives the first
  week of a patch is the one to build around.

## Check the date on every patch

The stats pages measure a stated data window, 2026-05-01 through 2026-08-23 at
the time of writing, and the window moves as new matches land. Every figure
drifts when it does. A guide or tier screenshot written for the 2026-07-28
patch describes a game that no longer exists, and the numbers quoted in this
article will age the same way, which is why each one carries its patch date.

That rule applies to us as much as to anyone. When you read one of our older
posts, check its date against the [patch list](/patches/) before you act on a
number in it; the live chips embedded in the guides update with the data, but a
quoted stat in prose is frozen on the day it was written.

The patch list itself proves why dates beat labels. One old entry is still
marked "(current)" even though the live patch is the 2026-08-22 Update,
preceded by the 2026-08-12 and 2026-07-28 updates. If you only read the tag,
you would study the wrong patch. When you cite a patch, cite its date.

## The cadence sets your practice window

The last three patches landed on 2026-07-28, 2026-08-12, and 2026-08-22, ten
days between the last pair and fifteen between the pair before that. The
rhythm is roughly two weeks, and the tracker is the source to trust for when
the next one lands.

That cadence is your practice budget. The first days after a patch are when the
tracker moves fastest and when a correct read earns the most, because the
players who adjust early pick into lobbies still running last patch's plans.
The final days before a patch are the worst time to invest in a borderline
pick, because the numbers behind it may be about to change.

Make the tracker's output concrete before you invest. When the patch page flags
a gainer, confirm the move at your band on the [heroes grid](/heroes/) using the
routine from our [rank bands guide](/blog/rank-bands-hero-choice/), then decide
whether the pick earns practice hours. A delta you cannot confirm at your own
bracket is someone else's meta.

Patch week rewards the players who read it early and read it at their own rank.
The tracker gives you both; the rest is queue time.
