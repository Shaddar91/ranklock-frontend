Open any hero page on RankLock and the first number you meet is the all-ranks
win-rate, one figure measured across every bracket in the data window. It is
the number most players check first, and for pick decisions it is the one most
likely to mislead you. The [heroes grid](/heroes/) carries a rank-band filter,
and that filter changes real answers. Two heroes from the current window show
how large the difference can get.

## The ladder you are filtering

Deadlock's ranked ladder runs twelve named tiers, from Obscurus through
Initiate, Seeker, Alchemist, Arcanist, Ritualist, Emissary, Archon, Oracle,
Phantom, and Ascendant up to Eternus, with subranks I through VI in each. The
highest badge on the ladder, Eternus VI, is badge 116. When you set the band
filter on the [heroes grid](/heroes/), you narrow every win-rate on the page to
games played by people ranked like you.

The same ladder shows up wherever the site brackets its numbers. The [items
page](/items/) groups its stats into bands that read Initiate through Alchemist
at the low end and Ascendant through Eternus at the top, so a bracketed number
anywhere on the site always refers back to these same twelve tiers.

That narrowing matters because an all-ranks win-rate blends lobbies that play
different games. A hero who punishes slow reactions can post a huge number in
low bands and an ordinary one in high bands, and the aggregate hides both facts
behind a single average.

Each hero page goes one step further and splits the hero's win-rate into low,
mid, high, and top brackets. That breakdown is where pick advice lives, because
a hero can hold steady across all four brackets or give points back as the
lobby gets stronger.

## Lash is the same pick everywhere

[Lash's bracket breakdown](/heroes/lash/) reads 50.8% in the low bracket, 51.1% in
the mid bracket, 50.6% in the high bracket, and 50.3% in the top bracket, as of
the 2026-08-22 patch. Four measurements, all inside a single point.

A flat line like that buys you two things. The all-ranks figure is honest,
since whatever band you play in, Lash's measured rate describes your lobbies.
And practice invested in him keeps paying: a hero whose kit works at Initiate
and still works at Eternus never punishes you for climbing. When the grid shows
a bracket-proof hero at a positive rate, you are looking at the closest thing
ranked offers to a safe default pick.

A line that flat also makes a good measuring stick. When another hero's numbers
swing across brackets, set them next to Lash's and you can see how much
movement a bracket-dependent hero actually shows.

Bracket-proof is not matchup-proof, though. The same page shows [Lash winning
45.0% into Seven across 430,906 matches](/heroes/lash/), his worst common pairing.
We cover how to read lines like that in the [matchups
guide](/blog/hero-matchups-101/).

## Haze is a different hero at high rank

[Haze](/heroes/haze/) carries a strong header line: 52.6% wins across 2,081,083
picks, a 2.80 KDA, and 44,793 average souls over the full window. Over two
million picks in a single window means the sample behind her numbers is
enormous, and the all-ranks figure still rewards her players.

Switch to the high bracket and the picture moves. Haze wins 50.0% there across
176,179 picks, as of the same patch. For the all-ranks figure to hold 52.6%
while the high bracket sits at 50.0%, her low and mid brackets have to run
above 52.6%. If you play at high rank, the headline number oversells her to you
by 2.6 points. If you play at low rank, it undersells her. The aggregate
describes almost nobody's lobby.

Two point six points sounds small until you price it. Over a hundred games, it
is the difference between winning about 53 of them and winning 50. Across a
season, that is the climb.

None of this makes Haze a bad pick at high rank. A 50.0% rate over 176,179
games is a large measured sample, and plenty of players win with her there. It
does mean the case for picking her has to come from your bracket's row, your
matchups, and your own comfort on the hero. The all-ranks column cannot make
that case for you.

## A working routine for pick decisions

Before your next session, run this sequence on the grid:

- **Filter first, then read.** Set the grid to your band before you look at any
  win-rate. The unfiltered column answers a question about the average lobby,
  and you do not play in the average lobby.
- **Read pick rate next to win rate.** [Seven currently leads the grid at 55.9%
  on a 3.7% pick rate, ahead of Victor at 55.5% and Graves at 55.1%.](/heroes/)
  A high rate on a small pick share marks a specialist choice; a 52.6% rate on
  more than two million picks marks a staple. Both can be correct picks, and
  they are different kinds of correct.
- **Re-check after every patch.** These figures drift as the data window moves.
  Everything quoted above describes the 2026-08-22 patch; after the next
  balance pass, run the filter again before you lock anything in.

## What the filter cannot do

A band-filtered win-rate is still a population average. It weights every game
in your bracket equally, the smurfs and the first-timers included, and it knows
nothing about which heroes *you* pilot well. Treat it as the prior that starts
your pick decision, then let your own results update it. If your band says a
hero wins 48% and you win 60% with her, your hundred games outvote the
population for your own draft.

The band filter takes ten seconds to set and routinely changes which heroes the
data recommends. Set it first.
