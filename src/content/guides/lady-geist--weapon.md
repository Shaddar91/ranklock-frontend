Pick this build when you expect the lane to become a long brawl you are allowed to stand in. Lady Geist spends her own health on Essence Bomb and Malice, and this page's answer is to let the pistol carry the fight while Life Drain pays the bill. Two published lists sit behind it: Dew, holding 20,557 favourites with an update of 2026-09-05, and a second list at 29,432 favourites updated 2026-09-09 whose title reads as a player handle, so this page cites it by its content alone. Both spend roughly a third of their slots on Weapon, a fifth on Spirit, and nearly half on Vitality.

## What this build buys, tier by tier

Open with the pieces both lists share: Monster Rounds for lane damage, Extra Regen and Radiant Regeneration to refill what your abilities cost. Radiant Regeneration is one of her signature purchases, bought at 6.3 times the roster-typical rate at Spirit tier 3, and the 2026-08-22 Update trimmed its heal on cast from 70 to 65, a change that reaches 27 of her 40 sampled builds. Add Bullet Resist Shredder or Suppressor for the first real spike, plus Kinetic Dash and Berserker. Cultist Sacrifice comes early too, and the note on Dew is blunt about its shelf life: sell it fourth or fifth.

Through the mid game, buy sustain before damage. Bullet Lifesteal and Spirit Lifesteal convert your pistol and spells into health, Spirit Resilience and Fleetfoot keep you in the fight, and Blood Tribute and Spellslinger start the payoff. Take Healbane the moment the enemy team heals through your trades.

Late, the climb to the tier-4 Weapon slots is where the bank goes: Mercurial Magnum, Spiritual Overflow, Crippling Headshot, Ricochet and Frenzy, with Leech and Siphon Bullets behind them. Frenzy leads this build's core items at a 54.8% win rate over 502,614 games, Spiritual Overflow holds 54.2% across 633,095, and Crippling Headshot sits at 53.5% over 295,905. The annotations add the judgment calls: rush Silencer when your team is behind, bring Spellbreaker against heavy spirit burst, and leave Cheat Death on the shelf when the enemy carries Curse.

## Ability order for this build

The two lists disagree on the opener, and both orders are defensible. Dew levels Essence Bomb first, then Life Drain, then Malice; the second list opens Life Drain ahead of Essence Bomb, with Malice third in both. Take Soul Exchange whenever it is offered.

Choose the Essence Bomb opener when the lane lets you lob into clumped Troopers and finish waves safely, since the bomb arms and detonates on a delay you can play around. Choose the Life Drain opener when the enemy trades constantly, because the tether drains their health into yours and covers what Bomb and Malice spend. Malice comes third but decides fights: its shards slow the target and amplify everything you do to them, gun damage included, so mark whoever you are shooting. Soul Exchange swaps health totals outright, which makes it the out when a duel goes wrong and the finisher when you are the one ahead.

## When to pick this over the standard build

The standard Lady Geist page leans on spirit, and her hero-wide item board shows it, with Spirit Burn at a 56.6% win rate across 2,627,891 games on top. Pick the weapon build when the enemy draft lets you stand close and shoot: the gun keeps working while Essence Bomb is on cooldown, and Life Drain plus lifesteal items turn your health bar into a resource you spend twice. Stay on the spirit page when your team needs the bomb's reach and you will not get to hold a tether.

Either way, play the early game like it is yours. Lady Geist wins 55.1% of matches that end inside 30 minutes, 50.5% in the 30-to-45-minute band, and 48.3% of longer games, so bank the lane lead into Guardians and the urn before her curve goes flat.

## Matchups this build changes

The matchup board is hero-wide, and it marks the lanes where your tether loses value. Victor holds her to a 45.9% win share over 314,337 matches and Seven to 46.0% over 352,153, with Graves at 46.8% over 298,054 behind them; the counter table confirms all three at 43.1%, 45.6% and 44.7% over their own samples. Kelvin and Ivy sit just under even. Against these, expect the enemy to break your tether on terrain and bring anti-heal early, so move Healbane and Silencer up your own list and hold Soul Exchange for the moment they commit.

Press the lanes the board favours. Sinclair concedes a 56.5% win share over 120,414 matches, Venator 55.9% over 352,832, and Mina 55.7% over 322,628, with Bebop and Silver close behind. In those games the brawl this build wants is one the enemy cannot leave.

## The numbers behind this build

Both lists land in the same place: about a third of their items and souls in Weapon, a fifth in Spirit, and the rest in Vitality. That is a weapon lean on a survival chassis, and it is why the page plays as a brawler with a pistol instead of a caster with a bomb.

The core items back it up. Frenzy posts a 54.8% win rate over 502,614 games, Spiritual Overflow 54.2% over 633,095, Crippling Headshot 53.5% over 295,905, Silencer 53.0% over 309,573, and Ricochet 52.8% over 212,335, while Spellslinger's 50.2% comes on a 2,588,008-game sample, the largest here. Mystic Regeneration at 7.8 times and Radiant Regeneration at 6.3 times the usual rate are her two signature purchases. On the 2026-08-22 Update she went unnamed, but the Radiant Regeneration heal cut from 70 to 65 touches 27 of her 40 sampled builds, and Restorative Locket's drop from 10% to 8% spirit resistance reaches 2 of them.
