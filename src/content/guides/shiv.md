## Who plays Shiv, and why

Take Shiv into a lane you can hold alone, hunt whoever walks without a partner, and spend the lead the moment you have one. Match length barely moves him: he wins 48.5% of games settled inside 30 minutes, 47.4% of those running 30 to 45 and 48.4% past 45, a spread of 0.1 across the whole curve. No hour-mark spike is coming to rescue a Shiv who fell behind at fifteen.

His body explains the ceiling. At 830 HP on 3 stamina and 6.5 m/s he is not quick enough to walk out of a fight he misjudged, and Bloodletting is the only thing standing between that and a death: it defers part of what lands on him and lets him wipe some of it away later. Carrying that debt while you finish the kill is the entire hero.

## How to play Shiv as an Offensive initiator

Lead with Slice and Dice. The dash deals damage and leaves the target with less spirit resistance, so it makes every knife after it hurt more, and it is the only closing tool he owns against someone already walking backwards.

Then bleed them and be patient. Serrated Knives stack, and a fresh hit refreshes and deepens what is already on the target, so the right instinct against a retreating enemy is another knife rather than a melee swing into a parry. Killing Blow charges its Rage off damage, which means the brawl you are already winning is what loads the ultimate; leap when the execute is genuinely in range, not when the bar merely looks full.

Press Bloodletting before you commit, not after you are low. It postpones damage instead of erasing it, so the value comes from choosing when the bill arrives. Open, take the hits you were always going to take, and clear part of the debt once their follow-up has been spent.

## How to play Shiv as a Damage carry

Buy regeneration before you buy amplification. Mystic Regeneration goes into his builds 6.9 times more often than the roster average and Radiant Regeneration 6.5 times, and both exist to keep him on the map farming, which is the only route a flat-curve hero has to his expensive slots on time.

The item table then pays for staying alive rather than for burst. Scourge reads 57.4% across 1,734,423 games on him and Ethereal Shift 56.9% over 1,773,380; Mystic Reverb, sitting on the largest sample of the ten at 2,676,472 games, reads 56.5%. Witchmail at 56.2% over 1,365,160 and Spirit Burn at 55.9% over 326,122 round out the shortlist worth building toward.

Read two of the ten carefully instead of copying them. Spellslinger's 56.5% comes from 10,409 games and Divine Barrier's 57.2% from 52,919, against tables in the millions elsewhere on the same page. Treat both as a hint about what works, not as an instruction.

## Abilities: what to level and when

All five lists agree on the order, which almost never happens: Serrated Knives, then Slice and Dice, then Bloodletting, with Killing Blow last. Take that seriously. The bleed is the damage, the dash is how the bleed arrives, and the ultimate needs no early point because Rage comes from fighting rather than from levels.

Bloodletting is the point you are allowed to move. Hold it back against a lane that only pokes at you, and take it sooner against anyone whose opener is a stun, because deferring damage is worth most when the damage lands all at once.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Graves is the matchup that decides whether you get to play. Shiv takes 40.2% of 23,709 games against him in the counter table and 41.8% of 300,607 in the wider matchup sample. Seven is barely kinder at 41.0% over 314,895 games, and Paige sits at 43.1% over 23,714.

All three beat the same habit. They keep damage on you straight through Bloodletting, and the deferred half still comes due, so the deferral buys a window rather than a rescue. Against them, take the fight only once part of the debt is cleared, and farm the lane they are not in instead of trying to solve the matchup head on.

Buy the escape early against the other three and pick your moment rather than forcing one, because none of them has to chase you, which removes the exact mistake Shiv's kit is built to punish. Victor sits at 43.6% over 336,602 games, McGinnis at 43.2% over 180,706 and Kelvin at 44.2% over 161,628.

The other end of the table is where the pick earns itself: 53.0% of 130,256 games against Sinclair, 51.8% of 343,649 against Venator, 51.3% of 359,217 against Bebop, 51.1% of 345,667 against Mina and 50.7% of 290,949 against Silver.

## Items that go well

Buy the exit before the amplifier. Ethereal Shift at 56.9% over 1,773,380 games is what lets him hold Bloodletting's debt through somebody's burst window, and Scourge at 57.4% over 1,734,423 is what turns the first kill into the second. Mystic Reverb and Witchmail belong in the same conversation once the slots exist.

Do not plan around a duo. His ally table gives nothing to lean on: Lash and Wraith both read 49.6%, over 22,259 and 20,704 games, and Haze 48.1% over 20,781. Play him as a hero who makes his own picks and expects the team to arrive afterwards.
