## Who plays Paige, and why

Pick Paige when your team needs a lane won early and a fight decided before anyone reaches their expensive slots, and treat the first twenty minutes as the match. She wins 59.6% of games settled under 30 minutes, 54.7% of those running 30 to 45 and 52.6% past 45, a drop of 7.0 points that says a Paige lead has a shelf life. At 680 HP and 6.9 m/s she also has no way to survive being wrong about where to stand.

Her kit is a lane bully wearing a support's coat. Bookwyrm sends a dragon forward for spirit damage and leaves a burning trail behind it. Plot Armor puts a barrier on an ally, and while that barrier holds they hit harder with their gun. Captivating Read lands on a delay: the area slows first, then deals spirit damage and locks whoever is still in it. Rallying Charge crosses the whole city, healing allies and damaging enemies, and it grows stronger the further it has run.

## How to play Paige as a Support

Put Plot Armor on whoever is winning, not on whoever is dying. The barrier hands its target weapon damage for as long as it lasts, so it converts a teammate who is already trading into one who wins the trade, while the same cast on a hero at low health buys a few seconds and no fight. Save it for a dive when you know one is coming — the standard read against her is to jump her the moment the barrier is spent somewhere else.

The shop agrees about what she is for. Guardian Ward is bought 7.7 times and Rescue Beam 5.9 times more often on Paige than the roster average, which is the widest gap of any purchase on the hero and the clearest statement of who she is playing the game for.

Cast Rallying Charge from far away. It hits harder with distance, so the correct habit is to start it from the lane you are not in and arrive with the damage already earned, rather than to hold it until you can see the fight.

## How to play Paige as a Damage carry

Throw Captivating Read where they are going. The slow arrives first and the damage and the immobilize arrive after it, so an area dropped on a moving hero catches nothing and an area dropped on the exit catches everything. Use Bookwyrm for the ground you cannot cover, aiming along their retreat so the burning trail is in front of them rather than behind.

Buy the two amplifiers with real tables under them before anything else: Spirit Burn wins 59.5% of 1,653,648 games on her and Escalating Exposure 58.6% of 1,710,327, the largest samples she has. Scourge tops her whole table at 61.0% over 226,689 games and belongs in the same plan.

Then take the survival that lets her keep casting. Diviner's Kevlar reads 60.4% over 172,581 games, Cheat Death 59.3% over 223,385 and Witchmail 59.2% over 236,106, three rows within about a point of each other on comparable evidence.

## Abilities: what to level and when

Plot Armor first on four of the five lists. That is the barrier being treated as the lane, not as a rescue: an ally with weapon damage on top of a shield wins the early exchanges that decide whether her steep front half is worth anything.

Captivating Read follows on those same four, and Bookwyrm third. The fifth list inverts the top two and opens on Bookwyrm, which is the read for a lane you expect to hold alone rather than trade in.

Rallying Charge goes last on all five. That is not caution — the ultimate scales on distance travelled, so it is worth a point when there is a second fight somewhere else to reach, and that is a late-match condition rather than an early one.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Victor is the name both of RankLock's lists put first. She wins 46.2% of 255,417 matches against him and the counter rows confirm it at 47.3% over 27,751, her only row that both lists agree is losing by more than three points. Seven follows at 46.9% over 346,159 in the matchup table and 49.4% over 33,648 in the counter rows, with Graves at 48.9% over 345,616 and 49.0% over 31,260. Kelvin at 48.8% over 168,883 and Ivy at 49.1% over 254,362 finish the worst five.

The fix is the same against all of them and it is a timing rather than an item. Every one of those heroes wants her barrier spent before they commit, so hold Plot Armor through the first exchange, keep Captivating Read for the ground behind them rather than the ground under them, and accept a slower lane in return for an ally who is still alive when the delayed area lands.

What she beats, she beats comfortably. Paige wins 57.1% of 310,676 matches against Venator, 56.5% of 137,979 against Sinclair, 56.0% of 313,763 against Mina, 56.0% of 340,456 against Bebop and 55.7% of 232,955 against Shiv.

## Items that go well

Her best opponent is her worst partner, and that is worth knowing before the draft. Venator is the hero she beats hardest at 57.1%, and Venator is also the only reported ally she loses with: 49.4% across the 21,480 games they share, against Celeste at 55.2% over 20,267 and Drifter at 55.1% over 23,932. Two Paige-shaped picks on one team is not the same trade as one Paige facing that pick.

For the item slots, weight the rows by how many games are behind them. Spirit Burn at 59.5% over 1,653,648 and Escalating Exposure at 58.6% over 1,710,327 are the two she can plan around; Echo Shard at 58.7% over 583,176 and Lightning Scroll at 58.3% over 662,908 come next.

Mystic Reverb reads 59.5% across 569,438 games, and Magic Carpet is the thin outlier at 60.9% over 48,679 — a high number on a table small enough that it should change one slot, not the build around it.
