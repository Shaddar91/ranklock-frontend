## Who plays Seven, and why

Pick Seven when the enemy team keeps standing together, and spend your lead the moment you have one. He wins 59.3% of games decided under 30 minutes, 55.5% of those running 30 to 45 and 52.5% past 45 — a fall of 6.8 points from a very high start, which means his problem is never getting ahead, it is closing while he still is. At 730 HP and 6.7 m/s on 3 stamina he is not built to hold a losing fight open.

Every ability wants more than one target. Lightning Ball travels down a line and slows as it damages whatever it passes through. Static Charge is a delayed charge placed on a hero that stuns and damages the ground around them when it goes off. Power Surge puts shock damage on his bullets that bounces off the body he hits. Storm Cloud expands around him and needs line of sight to reach anything.

## How to play Seven as an Offensive initiator

Cast Storm Cloud where nothing is in the way. Line of sight is the ability's only real rule, and it is the difference between an ultimate that decides a fight and one that empties itself into a wall — so the cast is a positioning question you answer before the fight rather than a button you press inside it. High ground, an open crossing or a room you have already cleared all count; a corner does not.

Expect to be interrupted, and plan the opener around that. The obvious answer to him is to save hard control for the moment the storm starts, so the ultimate goes out either when their control is spent or when your team is close enough to punish whoever spends it.

Static Charge is what keeps them inside the radius. It sits on a hero, then stuns and damages the ground where they stand, so put it on the enemy least able to leave rather than on the one you most want dead, and let the stun do the fencing your positioning cannot.

## How to play Seven as a Damage carry

Shoot into the crowd, not at the target. Power Surge makes his bullets bounce shock damage off whoever they hit, so the correct habit is to fire at the closest enemy in a group and let the proc spread, which is the same instinct that makes him fast at clearing waves and camps.

Lightning Ball is a line, so aim it along the lane rather than across it — it slows what it damages, and everything it passes through is more value from one cast.

Buy duration and reach before raw amplification. Greater Expansion wins 58.3% of 3,116,091 games on him, Spiritual Overflow 58.2% of 4,105,658 and Boundless Spirit 58.0% of 3,832,936: the three largest tables the hero owns, and all three of them buy more of the area he is already standing in. Indomitable reads 59.3% over 2,004,316 games and Mystic Slow 57.9% over 1,298,002.

Two purchases mark out his players. Lightning Scroll goes into Seven builds 3.9 times more often than the roster average and Spirit Lifesteal 3.4 times — one adds another source of damage that does not need aiming, the other pays him back for standing in the middle of what he cast.

## Abilities: what to level and when

Storm Cloud last on every list that records an order. That is unusual enough to be worth stating plainly: the ultimate is the reason to pick him and it is nobody's first point, because the three basic abilities all farm and it does not.

The first point splits three ways. Two lists open Power Surge, one opens Static Charge and one opens Lightning Ball. Power Surge first is the farming line and the one the most favourited set takes — it makes the gun clear waves and camps, which is how a front-loaded hero converts an early lead into souls rather than into risk.

Static Charge first is the lane-kill line and Lightning Ball first the safe poke line. Both are defensible; neither farms as fast, and on a hero whose best bracket is the shortest one, farming speed is the argument that usually wins.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

No hero on the roster wins the average match against him, and two still beat him where the evidence is tightest. His worst matchup rows are Victor at 50.3% over 396,201 matches, Graves at 51.4% over 456,119, Ivy at 52.6% over 309,994, Kelvin at 52.8% over 189,521 and Haze at 53.0% over 557,885 — every one of them a win. The confirmed counter rows tell a different story: Victor at 48.0% over 33,179 games and Graves at 49.5% over 34,384 are both losing, with Paige at 50.6% over 33,648.

Trust the tighter rows when the two disagree. A 50.3% matchup row on a hero who wins most of his games is a losing lane dressed as a winning one, and Victor and Graves are where his real trouble is.

Against both, the storm is the thing to protect. Do not open with it into a hero who can answer the channel, keep Static Charge for the moment they commit rather than the moment they appear, and let the front-loaded curve do the rest: his best five rows are Sinclair at 60.2% over 178,736 matches, Silver at 59.7% over 332,162, Venator at 59.4% over 544,910, Rem at 59.4% over 403,461 and Bebop at 59.3% over 450,179.

Three allies clear RankLock's reporting floor and all three want the same fight. Drifter wins 56.1% of the 22,680 games they share, Rem 52.8% of 20,039 and Bebop 52.1% of 24,293 — heroes who hold a group in place, which is the only condition under which an expanding storm is worth its cooldown.
