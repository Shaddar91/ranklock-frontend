## Who plays The Doorman, and why

Pick The Doorman when your team has a fight it wants and no way to get to it, and spend the early game buying cooldowns rather than looking for kills. He wins 41.2% of games decided inside 30 minutes, 45.8% of those running 30 to 45 and 48.1% past 45, a climb of 6.8 points, so the version of him that matters is the one with items.

He is the fastest hero in this batch at 7.9 m/s, on 755 HP and 3 stamina, and the speed is not there to escape. It is there so his doors and his cart end up where the fight is going rather than where it has been. Every ability he owns moves somebody, including him.

## How to play The Doorman as a Support

Place Doorway for your team's next move, not your last one. The pair of linked doors passes players and most projectiles, so it is a rotation your team can take together and a firing line at the same time. The best use is usually boring: a door from your safe side of an objective onto the flank your team is about to want.

Luggage Cart alt-casts on an ally, and that is the cast most Doorman players forget. Sent at an enemy it damages and drags them along its path; sent at a teammate it moves the person who needs to arrive. Decide which of those the fight needs before you press it, because both are the same cooldown.

Call Bell is a support ability disguised as poke. The delayed blast cuts accuracy and movement speed, so dropping it on the enemy who is about to shoot your carry is worth more than dropping it on the one you would rather kill.

## How to play The Doorman as a Damage carry

Spam Call Bell from the first minute and let the wave clear itself. It is the ability his shopping is built around, which is why cooldown and area purchases show up before damage on every list, and it is how a hero with a bad early win rate still farms through it.

Buy area and repetition first. Vortex Web goes into his builds 5.5 times more often than the roster average and Cheat Death 3.2 times, and the item table pays for the same idea: Mystic Reverb reads 57.1% across 953,812 games on him and Spirit Burn 55.1% over 1,517,642, his two largest samples by far.

Escalating Exposure at 55.9% over 122,385 games is the purchase that turns repeated casts into real damage, and Magic Carpet at 58.9% over 46,736 is the highest rate on his page with a table worth reading. Scourge's 59.2% sits on 26,301 games, so take it as a hint about direction.

## How to play The Doorman as an Offensive initiator

Hotel Guest removes a hero from the fight; it rarely kills one. It sends them to the hotel and makes them walk back taking spirit damage, so the question before you cast is what your team does with the numbers advantage while they walk. Cast it on the enemy whose absence changes the fight, then commit immediately.

Open objectives with the cart rather than the ultimate. Luggage Cart drags whoever it catches along its line, which breaks a defensive formation better than any single pick does, and the enemy answer is simply to spread out along that line before it arrives.

Guard both ends of your own door. The same passage that lets your team through lets theirs, and anyone who checks both endpoints before entering has taken most of the surprise out of the ability.

## Abilities: what to level and when

Four of the five lists take Call Bell, then Luggage Cart, then Doorway, with Hotel Guest last. The single exception swaps the middle two, taking Doorway second. Both work, and the difference is what you want the lane to be: the cart is a farming and trading tool, the door is a rotation you can start earlier.

Call Bell first is not in dispute anywhere, and it should not be. Its cooldown carries his laning, his wave clear and his harass, and the whole early item list exists to press it more often.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Lash is the one to plan for. The Doorman wins 44.3% of 20,619 games against him on the counter endpoint, the only row there under half besides Bebop at 49.4% over 23,406. A hero who arrives from above does not care which door you opened.

Use the door to leave rather than to commit against the worst three of the wider table, and let the cart and the bell do the damage from a distance you can hold. Victor sits at 44.1% over 180,737 games, Seven at 44.3% over 274,301 and Graves at 45.6% over 250,103, and each of them wins the fight your doors created.

Keep moving against the last two of the five, because McGinnis at 46.1% over 119,859 games and Kelvin at 46.2% over 113,000 both punish a Doorman who stands still. At the other end he takes 55.1% of 104,123 games against Sinclair, 54.6% of 247,106 against Venator, 53.8% of 175,937 against Silver, 53.4% of 257,689 against Mina and 53.1% of 165,565 against Shiv.

## Items that go well

Start with the two purchases that make him cast more often, then add the ones that make each cast hurt. Mystic Reverb at 57.1% over 953,812 games and Spirit Burn at 55.1% over 1,517,642 are the backbone, Escalating Exposure at 55.9% over 122,385 the amplifier, and Magic Carpet at 58.9% over 46,736 the mobility his doors already suggest he wants.

Answer a spirit team with Diviner's Kevlar at 54.7% over 26,801 games or Witchmail at 54.0% over 147,044. No ally clears the 20,000-game floor on his synergy endpoint, so there is no measured duo to draft around — build him for the fight your team is already trying to have.
