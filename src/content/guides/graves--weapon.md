Pick this build when you want Graves's pistol to lead the fight and her summons to clean up behind it. Essence Theft steals weapon damage and spirit resist as her shots connect, so a gun-first Graves grows stronger through every trade that drags on, while Jar of Dead and Grasping Hands keep the lane pinned. The kit is unchanged; what changes is where the souls go. The list behind this page, FaxFox Graves, holds 22,494 favourites and an update date of 2026-09-05, and it puts more than half of its souls into Vitality items while Weapon outspends Spirit several times over.

## What this build buys, tier by tier

Open with Restorative Shot. It is Graves's signature early purchase, bought at 3.7 times the roster-typical rate at Weapon tier 1, and its sustain lets you keep firing through the long trades Essence Theft wants. Pair it with Mystic Shot for lane poke, then round out the opening with Grit, Spirit Shielding and Stamina Mastery so you can stand in front of the Trooper wave while Grasping Hands works.

Through the mid game the list adds Titanic Magazine and Split Shot for raw gun output, Ricochet for fights where enemies stack together, and Siphon Bullets with Leech for sustain that grows with your damage. Heroic Aura is the signature mid-game slot, bought at 5.2 times the roster-typical rate at Weapon tier 3, and it pays the whole team for the front line you are already holding.

Late, save for the expensive Weapon slots instead of spreading souls thin: Spellslinger, Armor Piercing Rounds and Capacitor head the list's late section, with Toxic Bullets and Silencer as the flex picks. Spellslinger posts a 61.3% win rate across 27,240 games among this build's core items, the strongest weapon-core figure on this page, and Silencer sits at 60.6% across 779,203. The jump from mid-game prices to the top Weapon tier is where this build's bank goes once the lane is won, so do not sit on unspent souls while your team takes objectives.

## Ability order for this build

Level Jar of Dead first, Grasping Hands second, Essence Theft third, and take Borrowed Decree whenever it comes up. The jar is your wave control: every nearby death feeds it, and the thrown Deadheads chase whoever Grasping Hands catches, which is why the hands come second. A held target eats pistol rounds while the rift and its Ghoul pile on.

Essence Theft comes third in the order but carries this build. Each point makes your gun drain more weapon damage and spirit resist over the course of a fight, so once two points are in, extend every trade instead of backing off. Borrowed Decree is a long summon window; drop the gravestone where the enemy has to fight through the ghouls, at a Guardian siege or across the mid-boss pit.

## When to pick this over the standard build

Run this page when the enemy lane wants long trades over quick bursts. Essence Theft only pays in fights that last, so pick it into lanes where you can keep shooting; quick hit-and-run lanes favour the standard summon-heavy build. Pick it too when your team already brings spirit damage and needs a front line that shoots. This list spends over half its souls on Vitality, so you are still the wall your team hides behind, only a wall with a gun.

Whatever you pick, play for the early game. Her win share starts at 63.6% in matches finished before the 30-minute mark, slips to 57.4% in the 30-to-45-minute band, and settles at 53.8% in the longest games. That front-loaded curve rewards turning the lane lead into Walkers and the urn before the enemy catches up.

## Matchups this build changes

RankLock's matchup board is hero-wide, and it tells you where the gun pressure converts. Seven is Graves's worst matchup at a 48.7% win share over 478,262 matches, with Victor behind at 49.0% over 350,056, and the counter table confirms both, Victor at 49.1% and Seven at 50.3% across their confirmed samples. Against them, bank Jar charges before the fight starts and place Borrowed Decree where their team cannot simply walk out of it. Kelvin, Paige and McGinnis sit within two points of even.

Push hardest into Silver, where Graves holds a 60.1% win share over 319,698 matches, and into Venator at 60.0% over 448,877. Those are the lanes where an early gun lead snowballs. If the draft lets you pair with Victor, Celeste or Drifter, take it: those allies post 62.8%, 59.1% and 57.0% win shares alongside Graves, each over samples of at least 20,000 games.

## The numbers behind this build

Read the build's shape as a weapon lean on a vitality chassis: a little over a third of its items and souls are Weapon, under a tenth Spirit, and more than half Vitality. That split is why the page plays the way it does. You shoot to steal, and you stand still long enough for the theft to matter.

Among this build's core items, Spellslinger leads at a 61.3% win rate over 27,240 games, Silencer follows at 60.6% over 779,203, and Heroic Aura holds 57.9% across 6,392,978 games, the largest sample on the page. Mystic Shot and Ricochet sit at 57.4% and 57.6% over 5,631,826 and 2,317,043 games respectively. The 2026-08-22 Update never named Graves; the only change that touches this list is Restorative Locket dropping from 10% to 8% spirit resistance, an item carried by 6 of her 40 sampled builds.
