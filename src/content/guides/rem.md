## Who plays Rem, and why

Take Rem when your team has one hero worth keeping alive, and settle before your second item whether you are the one keeping them alive or the one doing the shooting. She wins 47.4% of games decided under 30 minutes, 47.2% of those running 30 to 45 and 49.1% past 45 — a climb of only 1.7 points, so there is no spike to wait for and no clock to beat. What decides a Rem game is which half of her you committed to, and the published lists disagree about that more than they disagree about anything else.

Her abilities are two jobs sharing a body. Pillow Toss is a throw with heavy knockback, and every hit it lands cuts the cooldown on everything else she has. Tag Along puts her down for a nap on an ally: a burst of healing scaled to the health they are missing, then regeneration on top. Lil Helpers sends out a helper that either farms boxes or trails a hero, handing them resistance and speed. Naptime is a piercing gaze that slows and blocks movement, then puts what it caught to sleep and detonates.

## How to play Rem as a Support

Nap on the teammate who is already hurt. The burst on Tag Along scales to missing health, so the same cast is worth a fraction on a full-health carry and a whole fight on one who has just been traded with — waiting is the skill, and topping people up is the habit to break.

The shop says this is the version most of her players buy. Healing Nova goes into Rem builds 8.8 times more often than the roster average and Guardian Ward 5.8 times, the two widest purchase gaps on the hero, and neither of them is a damage item.

Send Lil Helpers to a person once fights start. Farming boxes is what the ability does while nothing is happening; the resistance and speed it hands a hero is what it is for once something is, and that choice is worth more than any single item in this section.

## How to play Rem as a Damage carry

Lead with the gun and treat the healing as the thing you do between reloads. Her item table is a weapon table almost from top to bottom: Siphon Bullets wins 59.7% of 54,384 games on her, Silencer 57.6% of 130,667, Crippling Headshot 57.3% of 65,302 and Lucky Shot 56.2% of 149,510.

Read those numbers with their sample sizes in front of you. The two rows carrying real weight are spirit items — Mystic Reverb at 55.8% over 1,213,572 games and Spirit Burn at 55.7% over 1,266,706 — and both sit below every weapon row above on samples an order of magnitude larger. The honest reading is that the gun version wins more where it is played and is played by far fewer people.

Pillow Toss is what makes either version work. Each hit shortens every other cooldown she has, so landing throws is not chip damage, it is the resource that pays for the next heal or the next Naptime.

## How to play Rem as an Offensive initiator

Open with Naptime into a group that has already committed. The gaze slows and blocks movement before it sleeps and detonates, so it is a fight-starter with a windup, and the standard defence against it is to spread out before she casts — which means the value is highest on a team that is already stacked on an objective and lowest on one walking down a lane.

Follow it with throws rather than with a rush. The knockback keeps bodies out of your face and the cooldown refund means the opener pays for its own follow-up, which is the only reason a 680 HP hero at 7.2 m/s can afford to start a fight at all.

Buy the survival rows before the exotic ones. Cheat Death reads 57.4% over 290,950 games on her and Diviner's Kevlar 57.5% over 39,581 — the first of those is the one you can plan around.

Rem was not named in this patch and her exposure to it is small. Restorative Locket dropped from 10% Spirit Resistance to 8% and appears in 9 of her 40 sampled builds; Radiant Regeneration's heal-on-cast cut from 70 to 65 appears in 1. Neither is worth re-planning around.

## Abilities: what to level and when

Naptime last on all five lists, and everything above it argued over. Two lists open Pillow Toss, two open Lil Helpers and one opens Tag Along, which is the same split as the shop: the throw-first lists are the ones that end up buying guns, and the helper-and-nap lists are the ones that end up buying healing.

Pick the order that matches the build you intend to finish. Pillow Toss first if you want lane pressure and the cooldown refunds early, Lil Helpers first if you plan to farm and shepherd rather than trade, Tag Along first only when your lane partner is the reason your team is going to win.

The ultimate goes last regardless, and it is not a demotion. Naptime needs a grouped enemy team and a team of your own ready to spend the sleep, and neither of those exists in the first ten minutes.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Both of RankLock's lists name the same three heroes, and they are the three worst rows she has anywhere. She wins 40.6% of 403,461 matches against Seven, 41.3% of 332,673 against Victor and 42.2% of 428,813 against Graves; the confirmed counter rows read 40.2% over 28,137 against Victor, 40.5% over 36,475 against Graves and 41.0% over 30,469 against Seven. Kelvin at 44.2% over 210,497 and McGinnis at 44.4% over 233,945 finish the worst five.

There is no item that solves that list, so solve it with position. Everything she does — the nap, the helper, the gaze — asks her to be near a teammate and stationary, so the counterplay against these three is to do all of it a step further back than feels right and to let Pillow Toss create the space rather than answer for its absence.

Her good lanes are quiet ones rather than winning ones: Sinclair at 52.5% over 183,374 matches, Venator at 52.1% over 369,791, Mina at 51.3% over 381,394, Bebop at 50.9% over 421,877 and Silver at 50.7% over 331,460.

## Items that go well

The two heroes who beat her hardest are the two she most wants on her own team, and the numbers are not close. Victor is her best reported ally at 54.8% across 21,751 shared games and her worst opponent at 40.2%; Seven is second at 52.8% across 20,039 and beats her at 41.0%. Celeste is the third pairing that clears the floor and it loses, at 49.2% over 22,087.

That is a drafting instruction rather than a curiosity. If either of them is already on your team, Rem gains more than she gives up; if either is on theirs, the lane you are about to take is the worst one on her page.

For the slots themselves, buy Cheat Death at 57.4% over 290,950 games and Silencer at 57.6% over 130,667 as the two dependable rows, then Lucky Shot at 56.2% over 149,510. Frenzy at 58.2% over 13,092, Spellslinger at 56.9% over 19,581 and Diviner's Kevlar at 57.5% over 39,581 are the thin ones — worth a slot when the game asks for it, never worth building toward.
