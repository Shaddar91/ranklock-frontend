Pick this build when your draft can afford a quiet opening and you trust yourself to hit arrows from the back line. Grey Talon's gun lean plays through Rain of Arrows, a glide that grants bonus weapon damage and multishot, backed by a Charged Shot that pierces whatever lines up behind the first target. The published list behind this page is a 33,560-favourite gun build last updated 2026-09-05, and it commits half its item slots to Weapon while trimming Spirit to about a sixth. That is a sharper split than the hero's standard spirit-heavy page, and it asks you to farm your way to the items that make it sing.

## What this build buys, tier by tier

Open with the cheap gun pieces: High-Velocity Rounds, Rapid Rounds and Swift Striker for lane pressure, Long Range to stretch your threat, and Bullet Lifesteal so trades cost you less than they cost the enemy. Sharpshooter is the signature purchase on this hero, bought at 7.1 times the roster-typical rate at Weapon tier 3, and the list reaches for it early enough that you should plan your souls around it. Reactive Barrier and Fortitude cover you while you scale.

The core section is where the build commits. Glass Cannon headlines it, and the author's own note pairs it with Fortitude, whose health bonus covers the max-health cut Glass Cannon carries. Express Shot is the other named tech, and the same note frames it alongside Lucky Shot and Glass Cannon as the combination that turns lethal from the mid game on. Burst Fire, Crippling Headshot, Inhibitor and Vampiric Burst fill out the middle of the shopping list.

Late, the list closes with Spellslinger, and its annotation tells you how to hold the stacks: keep Charged Shot and Spirit Snare cycling, since both come back quickly. Indomitable, Spellbreaker and Plated Armor are the defensive finishers. On the numbers, Lucky Shot leads this build's core items at a 59.3% win rate over 215,274 games, Spiritual Overflow follows at 56.7% across 661,575, and Spellslinger holds 56.5% over 78,393. Crippling Headshot and Glass Cannon sit at 55.4% and 55.1% over 197,440 and 412,802 games respectively. Spend up to the top Weapon tier once the lane is done; hoarding souls through a teamfight loses more than any flex slot gains.

## Ability order for this build

Max Rain of Arrows first. The glide is the build: bonus weapon damage and multishot while airborne means every point turns your gun into a wider, harder-hitting weapon, and the list's whole item plan exists to feed that window. Charged Shot comes second, a held shot that pierces, and it doubles as your wave clear when Troopers stack up.

Spirit Snare takes points third. The trap arms, then curses, slows, silences and disarms whatever trips it, so place it on the path a diver has to cross and let them find it. Guided Owl goes in whenever it is offered; pilot it into a fight the enemy already committed to, because the explosion stuns and every kill it secures banks permanent spirit for the rest of the match.

## When to pick this over the standard build

Take the weapon page when your team does not need you to fight in the first ten minutes. Grey Talon is the roster's clearest late bloomer: a 42.1% win share in matches that end before the 30-minute mark, 48.0% in the half-hour-to-three-quarter-hour band, and 50.4% in games that run long, an 8.3-point climb from early to late. The gun lean doubles down on that shape, since its payoff items are the expensive ones.

Skip it when the draft calls for early pressure or when the enemy lanes two heroes who can close distance on you from the first wave. The standard spirit page fights earlier; this build trades that timing for a second half where your arrows, not your traps, decide the fight.

## Matchups this build changes

The matchup board is hero-wide, and it marks the divers. Seven holds Grey Talon to a 43.2% win share over 247,731 matches, Victor to 43.3% over 189,721, and Graves to 43.9% over 229,930, with Kelvin at 44.0% and McGinnis at 45.4% behind them. Bebop is the one name the counter table confirms, at 50.1% across 23,266 games, so respect the hook before you commit to a glide path. Against all of them, arm Spirit Snare on your own retreat line and spend Rain of Arrows after their engage lands.

Press the lanes the board favours. Sinclair concedes a 54.0% win share to Grey Talon over 99,677 matches, Venator 52.9% over 236,460, and Mina 52.5% over 230,527, with Pocket and Silver just behind. In those games you can glide forward early and make the enemy answer you.

## The numbers behind this build

The build's shape is a clean gun lean: half its item slots are Weapon, about a sixth Spirit, and a third Vitality, and the souls split reads nearly the same. You are buying arrows first and insurance second.

The hero's power curve is the steepest on this page: 42.1% early, 48.0% through the middle band, 50.4% late. Sharpshooter at 7.1 times the usual rate and Surge of Power at 2.4 times are the two purchases RankLock flags as signatures. Among core items, Lucky Shot's 59.3% win rate over 215,274 games tops the weapon set, and Spiritual Overflow's 56.7% comes on a 661,575-game sample. The 2026-08-22 Update never named Grey Talon; the single item change that grazes this list is Restorative Locket falling from 10% to 8% spirit resistance, carried by 6 of his 40 sampled builds.
