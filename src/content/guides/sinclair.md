## Who plays Sinclair, and why

Draft Sinclair when your team can afford to lose the opening exchanges, and play the lane to reach items rather than to win it. He wins 38.2% of games decided inside 30 minutes, 44.4% of those running 30 to 45 and 47.8% past 45 — a rise of 9.6 points, the steepest in this batch, and the reason a bad first ten minutes is survivable while a bad thirty is not.

Nothing about his body forgives a mistake. On 730 HP at 7.2 m/s with 3 stamina he is the frailest hero here, and his kit answers that with distance and misdirection rather than with resistance. Read the whole hero as a bet on reaching the late game with souls in hand.

## How to play Sinclair as an Offensive bruiser

Farm at range with Vexing Bolt and treat every cast as two decisions. The bolt grows in damage the further it travels, so the opening angle matters more than the aim, and the recast redirects it, which means a bolt sent down an empty corridor is still a bolt that can turn onto whoever steps out.

Spectral Assistant is the farming button and the escape in the same slot. The summon fires alongside him, which is why every list levels it first, and the recast swaps their positions — so place it where you would like to be standing a moment from now, not where the damage is best this second.

Buy for uptime before you buy for size. Echo Shard goes into his builds 2.5 times more often than the roster average and Knockdown 2.3 times, and Magic Carpet reads 54.2% across 643,572 games on him, the second largest table on his page. Mystic Reverb at 55.8% over 579,549 games is the highest number on any sample he owns above half a million.

## How to play Sinclair as an Offensive initiator

Open with Rabbit Hex when you have somewhere to put the follow-up. The hex covers an area and leaves whoever it catches unable to act at all, so it is a fight-starter rather than a chase tool, and its value is measured entirely in what your team does during it.

Audience Participation makes him a different hero in every match. He copies an enemy ultimate and holds it for a limited time, which turns the enemy draft into a shopping list: look at which of their ultimates you would most want to press, and position for the fight where pressing it decides something. That is a pre-fight decision, not a mid-fight improvisation.

The counter to all of it is somebody watching the Assistant. When they track the summon, the swap stops surprising anyone, so vary where you place it and accept that the second cast is sometimes just movement.

## Abilities: what to level and when

Every one of the five lists takes Spectral Assistant first, Vexing Bolt second, Rabbit Hex third and Audience Participation last. Unanimity across five independent authors is worth more than any one of them, so follow it unless you have a specific reason not to.

The reasoning behind the order is farming, not damage. The Assistant adds a second source of fire to every wave and every camp, and Sinclair's whole problem is arriving at the late game with enough souls to be the hero the curve promises. Rabbit Hex third is the compromise you make for that, and it is why his early ganks are weaker than his kit looks.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Read his matchup table honestly, because he does not have a winning one. His best is Venator at 48.7% over 167,726 games, tied with Mina at 48.7% over 169,038, then Bebop at 48.2% over 197,927, Pocket at 48.0% over 105,898 and Silver at 47.8% over 127,918. A hero who starts at 38.2% under 30 minutes owes that to the draft, not to you.

Seven is the worst of them at 39.8% over 178,736 games, with Victor and Graves both at 40.2% over 131,750 and 172,710. All three win by pressing him before his items exist, so the answer is the same in each case: take the safer lane, give up the early trade, and let the Assistant hold the wave while you farm behind it.

Do not spend the draft hunting a counter-pick answer. McGinnis at 40.5% over 82,521 games and Ivy at 41.2% over 115,452 fill out the five worst, and only Bebop clears the 20,000-game floor on the counter endpoint at all, at 48.4% over 20,689 games. Most of what happens to Sinclair happens before anyone counters him.

## Items that go well

Buy survival first, because the hero cannot use damage he does not live to spend. Cheat Death reads 55.9% across 117,261 games on him and Diviner's Kevlar 55.8% over 393,421, and both are the difference between a copied ultimate and a wasted one.

Then take the spirit line. Spirit Burn at 54.1% over 1,075,177 games is his largest sample by a wide margin, Mystic Reverb at 55.8% over 579,549 the strongest of the big tables, and Spiritual Overflow at 54.7% over 121,602 the one that pays for casting more often. Scourge's 56.3% and Spellslinger's 58.4% look better than either, but they rest on 44,259 and 10,846 games; treat them as a direction rather than a decision.

His synergy endpoint returns nothing above its 20,000-game floor, so no duo has been measured. Pick him for the game plan, not for the partner.
