## Who plays Viscous, and why

Take Viscous when your team has a carry worth protecting and no way to stop the hero diving them. Do not take him to win a lane. He wins 46.4% of matches decided under 30 minutes, 48.7% of those running 30 to 45 and 48.3% past 45, a rise of 2.0 that puts his value after the lane, in a fight with more than two people in it.

Play the early game to survive it, not to trade. On 780 HP at 7.2 m/s with 3 stamina he has the health to eat a mistake, and the goo does the pressure that his gun does not.

## How to play Viscous as a Support

Spend The Cube on somebody else. It makes its target damage-proof, freezes them in place and regenerates them, which turns the moment your carry gets caught into four seconds where the enemy has nothing to shoot. Cube the diver's target, not the diver, and use the same button on yourself only when the alternative is dying.

Read the fight for the save before you read it for damage. A cubed ally cannot act either, so the good uses are the ones that outlast a cooldown: a Bebop hook that has already landed, an ultimate that has already started, a burst window that ends before your ally comes back out. The bad use is cubing a teammate who was about to win the trade anyway.

Puddle Punch is the second half of the job. The fist knocks enemies up and slows them, and while they are in the air your team can hit them with anything that needs a stationary target, so call it before your Seven or Bebop commits rather than after.

## How to play Viscous as a Damage carry

Farm with Splatter and stop thinking of it as a nuke. The ball damages what it hits and leaves slowing puddles behind, so it clears a wave, holds a doorway and slows an escape at the same time, and the puddles are what make the next Puddle Punch land.

Buy spirit early, because his players do. Spirit Strike goes into his builds 3.7 times more often than the roster average and Mystic Burst 2.1 times, both cheap tier-one purchases that turn a punch into a real hit rather than a displacement.

Then commit to the long game the numbers describe. His win rate rises past the half-hour mark and the items that carry it are expensive, so the correct middle game is one where you take farm you can reach safely and trade only when The Cube or the ultimate is available.

## How to play Viscous as an Offensive initiator

Roll in last, not first. Goo Ball stuns what it hits, bounces off walls and gives him heavy resists while it lasts, which makes it an excellent way to arrive on a fight your team has already joined and a poor way to announce one alone. Line the roll so it clips two heroes rather than chasing one.

Aim for the group that cannot spread out. The straight lines Goo Ball bounces along are answered by enemies who stand around corners from each other, so pick the fight in a corridor, a pit or on an objective they cannot leave, and open with Puddle Punch so they are still airborne when you arrive.

## Abilities: what to level and when

Pick your first point from the lane you drew. Three of his five lists open on Splatter and two open on Puddle Punch, and that is a real choice rather than noise: Splatter first is the safer lane, clearing troopers and holding ground from range, while Puddle Punch first is the aggressive one that needs a teammate to punish what you knock up.

Take The Cube second or third, never last. Two lists put it second and three put it third, and all five leave Goo Ball for the ultimate points because the roll is a tool you use once a fight rather than a button you spam.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Seven, Victor and Graves take more from him than anybody else, and both of RankLock's tables agree on the worst of the three. He wins 41.4% of 209,022 matches against Seven, 42.1% of 183,728 against Victor and 42.2% of 199,375 against Graves; the counter endpoint reads Graves 42.7% over 20,551 games, Ivy 45.1% over 22,039 and Celeste 46.2% over 22,755. McGinnis at 44.1% over 114,301 and Kelvin at 44.3% over 109,756 complete the list.

All of them out-range him or out-burst him before he arrives. Hold The Cube for their opener rather than spending it early, keep a wall between yourself and Graves, and take fights where their damage has to come through a doorway you are already standing in.

Pick him into the heroes who have to walk to you. He wins 53.3% of 92,706 games against Sinclair, 51.6% of 222,191 against Venator, 51.3% of 185,822 against Mina, 51.1% of 250,443 against Bebop and 51.0% of 155,766 against Silver.

## Items that go well

Start with the two items that make his spirit stick. Shadow Weave reads 59.1% across 67,068 games on him and Spellslinger 58.4% across 295,665, by far the larger sample of the two, and both pay a hero whose damage arrives as abilities rather than bullets.

Spend the next slots on the body that survives the roll. Colossus sits at 57.3% over 194,580 games, Witchmail at 56.4% over 550,753 and Silencer at 55.8% over 99,703, which together answer the burst that kills him and the healing that outlasts him. Glass Cannon at 57.3% over 81,161 is the greedy alternative to Colossus, and Hunter's Aura at 56.6% over 95,045 is the one purchase on the list that helps everyone standing near him.

Treat the small samples as leads. Frenzy shows 56.4% across 44,666 games, Berserker 56.5% across 28,288 and Arctic Blast 55.4% across 34,982, all far below the sample size behind Spellslinger. RankLock publishes no ally pairing for him above its 20,000-game threshold, so pick him for the fight you expect rather than for the teammate you already have.
