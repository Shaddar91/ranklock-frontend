## Who plays Kelvin, and why

Take Kelvin when your team keeps losing fights it should have won on numbers, and play him as the hero who decides where the next one happens. He is a heavy body — 880 HP, 6.7 m/s, 3 stamina — and his win rate barely moves with match length: 53.5% under 30 minutes, 55.1% from 30 to 45 and 53.2% past 45. There is no clock to beat and no spike to reach, only ground to take.

Every ability puts something on the floor and makes the enemy walk around it. Frost Grenade heals allies where it lands and damages and slows everyone else. Ice Path lays a floating trail he and his team can ride, and the enemy can ride it too. Arctic Beam is a sustained beam whose slow and fire-rate loss ramp the longer it holds, and it can claim souls. Frozen Shelter is a dome: allies regenerate inside it, enemies slow, and objectives inside it go invulnerable.

## How to play Kelvin as a Support

Drop Frozen Shelter on the thing you are defending, not on the teammate you are rescuing. Objectives inside the dome go invulnerable, so putting it over a pressured Guardian buys a rotation that no amount of healing buys. Saving a body is the second-best use of that button and it is the one most players reach for first.

The support half of the shop is reach and cooldowns rather than healing. Kelvin players buy Rescue Beam 5.0 times and Healing Tempo 3.9 times more often than the roster average, and Superior Duration wins 59.4% of the 1,887,467 games it appears in on him. Each of those extends how long a piece of ground stays yours.

Frost Grenade is lane sustain and lane poke in the same throw, so aim it where both halves land: on the teammate who is trading, with the enemy standing in the same circle.

## How to play Kelvin as a Damage carry

Hold Arctic Beam on one body and refuse to move it. The slow and the fire-rate loss ramp while the beam stays on target, so the whole ability is a bet that you can keep pointing at one person longer than they can stand it. Sweeping across a fight resets the ramp and throws the cast away.

The shop follows that idea, and two of the five published lists say so in their names: `Beam build [400 DPS]` from Account 1310475170 at 3,021 weekly favourites, and `BEAM BULLY HIGH DPS` from Account 108697811 at 49,302 favourites. Both open on spirit stats and cooldown reduction instead of survivability.

Buy the amplifiers in order of sample size, not headline. Spirit Burn wins 63.3% of 1,221,911 games on him, Escalating Exposure 58.8% of 2,689,541 and Transcendent Cooldown 58.4% of 1,445,974. Diviner's Kevlar reads 62.5% across only 25,871 games and Echo Shard 60.2% across 11,107, so both are leads rather than rules.

## How to play Kelvin as an Offensive initiator

Open with Ice Path, not with the beam. The trail hands your team a route over terrain the enemy is not watching, and it is the cheapest way to reach a fight from an angle nobody has covered. It carries enemies just as well, so never build a road into your own back line.

Then take the ground with Frozen Shelter and force the fight to happen inside it. Everyone who is not on your team slows in there, which turns a scattered engagement into a slow one you have already positioned for.

The lanes worth opening are the ones the numbers hand you before the fight starts. Kelvin wins 58.7% of 78,277 matches against Sinclair, 58.2% of 161,161 against Mina, 58.1% of 195,969 against Venator, 57.1% of 203,805 against Bebop and 56.6% of 98,856 against Holliday.

## Abilities: what to level and when

Arctic Beam first, and the lists nearly agree. Three of the four that record a point order open on the beam; the fourth, `Beam build [400 DPS]`, takes Frost Grenade first instead. All four then put Ice Path third and Frozen Shelter last.

Read the split as a lane question. Beam first when you are the one applying pressure, because the ramping slow is what turns a won trade into a kill. Frost Grenade first when you are being traded down, because the heal on your side of the circle is what keeps you in the lane.

Ice Path third is not a demotion. Its value is positional rather than numeric, so points spent there buy less than points in the two abilities that scale.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Three heroes beat Kelvin in RankLock's own matches, and all three do it by killing him before the ground matters. He wins 47.2% of 189,521 matches against Seven, 49.1% of 159,840 against Victor and 49.3% of 173,555 against Graves. McGinnis at 50.2% over 106,634 and Ivy at 50.5% over 146,514 sit right on the line.

Against those four, place the dome early rather than as a rescue. You lose the matchup on the opening exchange, not on the long fight, so make the opening exchange happen on terrain you prepared.

The counter endpoint tells the other half of the story: Kelvin's worst confirmed rows there are still winning ones, at 53.2% over 23,794 games against Infernus, 53.3% over 20,075 against Lash and 57.4% over 22,938 against Bebop. Read that as licence to take fights you would refuse on most heroes.

## Items that go well

Buy the two that pay a beam already held on target. Spirit Burn tops his table at 63.3% over 1,221,911 games and Mystic Reverb reads 62.8% over 566,559; both charge per tick rather than per cast. Witchmail follows at 62.2% over 300,934 and Infuser at 61.1% over 598,340.

Scourge at 61.7% over 105,205 games earns a slot when the enemy stacks bodies inside your dome. No ally pairing clears RankLock's reporting floor on him, so there is no partner to plan around — his value comes from the terrain, and that works with whoever is standing on it.
