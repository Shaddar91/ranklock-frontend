Pick this build when your carry is the win condition and your job is to pull them out of the fight alive. The Cube makes one ally damage-proof, regenerating and frozen in place, and this list builds around that button: it passed the support classification with 3 ally-targeted items and holds 62,624 favourites with an update on 2026-09-03. You trade the burst shopping of his tracked Spirit-Burst label for saves, team actives and a Goo Ball that starts the fight on your timing instead of ending it on theirs.

## What this build buys, tier by tier

Open with the lane kit: Headshot Booster for poke, which the annotation marks as sellable once slots tighten, High-Velocity Rounds upgrading into Express Shot for confirming and stealing soul orbs, and Mystic Burst for Splatter with Tankbuster as its printed upgrade path. Mystic Shot pairs with your alt-fire, and the notes point out it can trip Spellbreaker protection before you commit Splatter itself. Grit and Spirit Shielding hold you together, and Compress Cooldown with Mystic Expansion round the row out.

Mid game splits three ways. The damage track is Veil Walker, Superior Cooldown, Tankbuster, Spirit Snatch, Transcendent Cooldown and Boundless Spirit. The survival shelf is Reactive Barrier, Enchanter's Emblem, Metal Skin, Spirit Resilience and Counterspell. The support row is the build's reason to exist: Rescue Beam and Healing Nova for the saves The Cube cannot cover, with Stamina Mastery, Warp Stone, Fortitude and Majestic Leap for the repositioning after you roll in.

Late, take Greater Expansion, Spirit Burn, Mystic Reverb, Escalating Exposure and Infuser, then spend active slots on Guardian Ward, Cold Front, Arctic Blast, Vortex Web and Scourge, with Knockdown, Silence Wave, Cursed Relic and Ethereal Shift held for the enemy's own actives. The jump into these tier-4 slots is the widest soul gap the list asks of you, its version of the spike every expensive page pays, so bank through the mid game and finish a big active before your team touches the mid-boss.

## Ability order for this build

Level Splatter first, The Cube second, Puddle Punch third, and take Goo Ball at every ultimate point. Splatter opens because the goo is your lane presence: the ball damages, the puddles it leaves slow, and nearly every early item on this page feeds that button.

The Cube second because one point already delivers the whole save, an ally who is damage-proof, regenerating and frozen while the enemy's ult washes over them. Puddle Punch third; the fist knocks up, slows and hands your team air control, so whatever follows lands on a target still coming down. Goo Ball itself is your opener: roll in, stun on impact, bounce off a wall for the second angle, and let the ball's huge resists carry you back out.

## When to pick this over the standard build

Run this page when your draft has damage to spare and one carry worth freezing. Viscous climbs as games stretch, from a 46.4% win rate inside 30 minutes to 48.7% in the 30-to-45 band and 48.3% past 45, a 2.0-point gain from early to mid that fits a build whose best items are actives and saves. The longer the night runs, the more a Cube on the right ally decides the fight.

Stay on his burst lists when nobody on your team out-damages you. A support Viscous spends on saves for an ally who must exist, and if your four teammates cannot convert a five-versus-four while you hold their diver in stasis, the build's whole premise is gone. His signature purchases stay modest either way, Spirit Strike at 3.7 times the roster-typical rate and Mystic Burst at 2.1 times, both at Spirit tier 1, so the swap costs you nothing early.

## Matchups this build changes

Seven tops the bad rows at a 41.5% win share over 220,498 matches, with Victor and Graves both at 42.1% over 196,908 and 214,228, then McGinnis at 44.4% over 120,974 and Kelvin at 44.3% over 117,340. The counter table confirms Graves at a 42.3% win share over 20,215 games, Ivy at 45.1% over 20,339 and Vindicta at 46.1% over 20,423. The card on beating Viscous says to spread around corners so one Goo Ball line cannot hit everyone and to hold burst until The Cube is spent. Into these drafts, assume the enemy knows it: roll along walls instead of down lanes, and cube the target they actually committed their damage on.

His good rows are Sinclair at a 53.4% win share over 100,611 matches, Venator at 51.7% over 237,833, Mina at 51.5% over 199,799, Silver at 51.2% over 165,378 and Bebop at 51.0% over 269,345. No ally pairing clears the synergy table's 20,000-game floor for Viscous in this window, so judge the build by what it saves; there are no partner stats to lean on.

## The numbers behind this build

The support core reads weak on paper, and that is the honest headline. Among the build's core items on the item-win-rates endpoint with a 10,000-game floor, Spirit Rend posts a 48.6% win rate over 28,029 games, Healing Nova 48.0% over 187,114 and Spirit Shredder Bullets 47.9% over 33,597. The hero-wide board runs hotter, with Shadow Weave at 58.9% over 74,195 games, Colossus at 57.2% over 245,812, Witchmail at 56.5% over 712,925 and Arctic Blast at 55.5% over 42,689. Read the gap for what it is: the support core is a sacrifice you make for The Cube windows, not a stat-line upgrade, and the saves never show up in an item table.
