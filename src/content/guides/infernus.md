## Who plays Infernus, and why

Take Infernus when you want to be the hero the enemy cannot disengage from, and spend your lane looking for the trade rather than the farm. He wins 51.2% of matches decided under 30 minutes, 50.4% of those running 30 to 45 and 49.1% past 45, so the window where the pick is worth most is the one you are in right now. He has 830 HP, moves at 6.7 m/s and holds 3 stamina, which is enough to chase and not enough to escape a mistake.

Afterburn is the reason to keep firing: weapon hits build a burn on the target, and his abilities refresh its duration rather than replacing it. Napalm sprays an incendiary cone that damages, slows and coats whoever it touches. Flame Dash is a dash with slow resistance that leaves a burning trail behind him. Concussive Combustion detonates him on a delay for spirit damage and a stun, and the cast cannot be interrupted once it starts.

## How to play Infernus as a Damage carry

Land bullets, then keep landing them. Afterburn is a stacking commitment: the burn you started with your weapon is extended every time an ability touches the same target, so the correct pattern is gun into Napalm into gun, not ability into ability. An enemy who breaks contact for three seconds has undone most of your damage, which is why chasing is a real decision on this hero rather than a mistake.

Buy the anti-heal that stops the burn being erased. Infernus players take Spirit Rend 5.9 times more often than the roster average and Spirit Shredder Bullets 4.9 times, and the reason is structural: damage over time is the easiest damage in the game to heal through, so the item that cuts the healing is worth more than the item that adds a tick.

After that, the spirit line does the scaling. Spirit Burn wins 58.1% of the 878,106 Infernus games it appears in, Mystic Reverb 56.6% of 69,409, and Mercurial Magnum 55.8% of 1,126,264 — the biggest sample in his table and the item that keeps his weapon relevant while the burn does its work.

## How to play Infernus as an Offensive initiator

Flame Dash into the fight, not out of it. The slow resistance is what lets you arrive through the enemy's control, and the burning trail you leave behind holds the ground you crossed, so the dash is worth more used at the start of a fight than kept for the end of one. If you are saving it as an escape you have picked the wrong hero.

Concussive Combustion cannot be interrupted, which is the only reason it is safe to open with. Cast it standing in the middle of their group, because the delay means the enemy has time to leave and the stun only pays if enough of them are still there. Napalm the retreat path first and the cone's slow keeps them inside the radius long enough for the detonation to land.

Arctic Blast is the item that fits that plan, at 57.3% over 164,166 games on the hero, and Witchmail follows at 56.8% over 260,340. Both let you hold contact for the seconds your damage needs rather than trading once and leaving.

## Abilities: what to level and when

All five published builds put the first point in Afterburn: build 376685 from Account 393148161 at 8,490 weekly favourites, `gun-spirit V1 [by hlupik]` from Account 180316092 at 5,789, build 557267 from Account 1105000141 at 4,528, and both lifetime lists. Unanimous agreement across five authors is as strong as build evidence gets, and it says the same thing the kit does: the burn is the hero.

The lists split on second. Three of them take Flame Dash, and two — `gun-spirit V1 [by hlupik]` and `Zieth Infernus` — take Napalm. Choose on the lane. Flame Dash second when you are being harassed by a hero who can leave, because the dash is how you re-establish contact. Napalm second when you are contesting the wave and want the slow plus the coating to hold the enemy in your gun range. Concussive Combustion is fourth in every list.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Three heroes beat Infernus and all three do it by not being there. He wins 44.5% of 367,238 matches against Victor, 44.8% of 430,418 against Graves and 44.9% of 518,766 against Seven, and the counter endpoint puts Graves worst at 43.1% over 40,705 games, then Victor at 43.5% over 38,157 and Paige at 45.5% over 39,304. Kelvin at 46.6% over 212,785 matches and Ivy at 46.7% over 308,997 sit just behind.

Against all of them the fix is the same and it is not an item. Do not walk into the open to start the burn. Use Napalm from behind cover to force them off the ground they want, and hold Flame Dash until their control is spent, because arriving into a Kelvin or a Graves area with no dash left is how the matchup produces those numbers. The lanes to press are Sinclair at 55.5% over 164,610 matches, Venator at 53.4% over 483,873, Mina at 53.3% over 470,695, Bebop at 53.3% over 437,706 and Rem at 52.8% over 412,480.

## Items that go well

Spirit Burn at 58.1% over 878,106 games is the first real purchase and the one his own builders agree on. Healing Tempo follows at 56.7% over 375,007 and Silencer at 55.9% over 507,372, which answers the enemy holding the stun that would otherwise end a dash mid-air.

Two entries near the top are thinner than they look: Echo Shard reads 57.3% over 13,206 games and Spellslinger 57.0% over 60,788, so buy either for a reason rather than off the ranking. Crippling Headshot at 55.8% over 301,995 games is the gun-side answer to a healing team and pairs with the anti-heal you already wanted.

Play beside a hero who stops the enemy leaving. Infernus wins 56.3% of the 21,911 games he shares with Graves, 52.2% of 21,366 with Celeste and 51.0% of 21,421 with Mo & Krill, and every one of those three pins a target long enough for the burn to finish it.
