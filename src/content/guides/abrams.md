## Who plays Abrams, and why

Take Abrams when nobody on your team is willing to walk in first, and expect to spend the early game buying time rather than making highlights. He starts on 800 HP, moves at 6.4 m/s and holds 3 stamina, and his win rate climbs as matches run long: 48.4% under 30 minutes, 51.0% from 30 to 45, 52.0% past 45.

Everything in the kit rewards staying in contact. Siphon Life drains the enemies standing near you for spirit damage over time and heals a share of it back. Infernal Resilience turns damage you have already eaten into temporary regeneration. Shoulder Charge is a pull-dash that only stuns when the target hits a wall, and Seismic Impact leaps onto a group for spirit damage and an area stun. None of that works from range, so the decision you make on this hero is always the same one. Get close, and pick the ground first.

## How to play Abrams as an Offensive initiator

Choose the terrain before you choose the moment. A lane doorway, a juke room or the sewer pit around the mid-boss is worth more to you than open street, because Shoulder Charge without a wall behind the target is a shove with no stun on the end of it. Charge into geometry, land Siphon Life on whoever collapses in, then let your team arrive into a target that is already slowed down by its own healing being cut.

Seismic Impact is your second opener, not your escape. Use it when two or more enemies are grouped near an objective and your team is in range to follow, since the area stun is the only part of the kit that stops several people at once. The most-favourited weekly build on the hero, build 393691 from Account 393148161 at 7,187 weekly favourites, puts the first point into Shoulder Charge, and so does the second at 3,734 favourites from Account 324177406. If you plan to start fights, level the ability that starts them.

## How to play Abrams as a Tank

Front for someone. Your job in this version is to be the first body an enemy team has to deal with and the last one they manage to kill, and the item table backs that up. Colossus wins 58.4% of the 1,550,351 Abrams games it appears in and Witchmail 58.4% of 1,561,963. Buy them because you intend to stand in front of a Walker while your damage dealers finish it, not because you are already losing.

Hold ground rather than chasing. Siphon Life ticks on everyone inside it, so standing between the enemy team and the thing they want is worth more than a kill you dive for and lose. Anti-heal is the one purchase that undoes a tanking Abrams, because it cuts both the drain and the passive regeneration at once, and once both are off you are just a large target. When you see it bought, stop trading and rotate.

## How to play Abrams as a Damage carry

Play him as the damage in a slow team by leaning on melee. The two purchases that separate an Abrams build from the roster average are Melee Charge, bought 8.8 times more often on him than on the field, and Lifestrike at 6.0 times, and both convert punches into sustain instead of burst. Spirit items do the scaling. Escalating Exposure wins 61.0% of 692,782 games on the hero and Spirit Burn 60.1% of 128,746, both feeding the damage over time you already apply for free.

That plan needs the match to reach the expensive slots, which is what his curve is telling you. Do not force a mid-game fight you cannot fund. Farm the jungle boxes between waves, take the urn when it lands on your half, and let the 52.0% band past 45 minutes come to you.

## Abilities: what to level and when

The published builds split on the first point and agree on everything after. Both leading weekly builds go Shoulder Charge, then Infernal Resilience, then Siphon Life, then Seismic Impact. The lifetime leader inverts the opening pair to Infernal Resilience, then Shoulder Charge, then Siphon Life, then Seismic Impact, and build 273842 from Account 1536378615 at 2,527 weekly favourites goes Shoulder Charge, Siphon Life, Infernal Resilience, Seismic Impact.

Pick between them on your lane. Take Shoulder Charge first when the enemy laner has to stand near a wall to farm and you can convert that into a stun. Take Infernal Resilience first when you are the one being harassed and need the regeneration to hold the wave. Siphon Life ranks up well in either case because it is your only source of pressure on a group.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Three heroes beat Abrams on RankLock's own matches, and all three do it the same way. Against Seven he wins 44.9% of 322,211 matches, against Graves 46.0% of 318,034 and against Victor 46.1% of 327,948, and the counter endpoint confirms the same order at Graves 43.9% over 29,087 games, Victor 44.6% over 31,725 and Seven 45.2% over 27,860. The cause is on your side of the fight: Siphon Life pays you for committing and holding still, and all three punish exactly that.

Play those lanes by refusing to be the one who opens. Wait for their ability to be spent, then charge. Kelvin at 47.4% over 147,158 matches and Ivy at 47.9% over 225,241 sit just behind them for the same reason. The rows that go your way are Sinclair at 56.4% over 140,834 matches, Venator at 55.6% over 335,309, Mina at 55.1% over 296,168, Bebop at 54.4% over 379,285 and Silver at 54.4% over 264,461. Those are the lanes to charge into early rather than farm through.

## Items that go well

Buy the spirit line first if the game is going long. Escalating Exposure at 61.0% over 692,782 games and Spirit Burn at 60.1% over 128,746 are the two highest-winning items on the hero with a real sample behind them, and Scourge follows at 58.5% over 1,700,193. For the front-line half of the build, Witchmail wins 58.4% of 1,561,963 games and Colossus 58.4% of 1,550,351.

Pair yourself with someone who can finish what you hold. Abrams wins 51.9% of the 21,981 games he shares with Wraith, 51.8% of 20,913 with Haze and 50.6% of 21,112 with Infernus, and every one of those is a hero that needs its target pinned against a wall and unable to walk away.
