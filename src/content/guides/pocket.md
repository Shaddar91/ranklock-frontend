## Who plays Pocket, and why

Draft Pocket when your team can afford to lose a lane and needs somebody who can walk into a back line later, then spend the early game farming rather than proving a point. He wins 41.5% of games decided under 30 minutes, 44.9% of those running 30 to 45 and 49.2% past 45, a climb of 7.7 points, so nothing before the half-hour mark is supposed to look like it is working. He has 780 HP and moves at 7.2 m/s on 3 stamina, which is enough to leave a fight and never enough to stand in one.

The kit is built for arriving and leaving rather than for staying. Barrage is a channel that grows: every hero it hits stacks his damage for the rest of it. Flying Cloak throws a cloak he can teleport to. Enchanter's Satchel hides him inside a suitcase, and whatever is nearby when he steps out takes the damage. Affliction ticks passively on enemies near him and cannot land the kill itself.

## How to play Pocket as an Offensive initiator

Throw Flying Cloak where you want to leave from, not where you want to arrive. Every good Pocket play is an exit planned before the entry, and the cloak is the only part of the kit that lets you choose both ends of the trip; the standard answer to him is to hold a stun for wherever that cloak lands, so a cloak thrown behind the enemy team is a cloak thrown into their reaction time.

Enchanter's Satchel is the second half of the same idea. It removes him from the fight while the enemy is still looking for him and damages whoever stayed close when he comes out, which makes it both the escape and a reason for them to spread.

The shop confirms which part of the game his players spend souls on. Majestic Leap is bought 7.4 times more often on Pocket than the roster average, the widest gap on the hero by some way, and Cold Front 3.3 times — one buys the arrival, the other punishes the crowd that gathers to answer it.

## How to play Pocket as a Damage carry

Barrage rewards you for holding it, which is the hardest thing about the hero. Hero hits stack his damage as the channel runs, so the fight is decided by whether you can keep sight of one body long enough for the stacks to matter, and the counter to him is exactly that: break the line and the channel was worth nothing.

That is why the sustain rows sit at the top of his item table rather than the damage ones. Cheat Death wins 56.9% of 601,832 games on him, Spiritual Overflow 56.4% of 693,257 and Diviner's Kevlar 56.2% of 849,225. Ethereal Shift is the largest sample he has at 55.2% over 1,057,556 games, and it buys the same thing every other row here buys: another second of channel.

One published list rejects the whole plan and it is worth reading. `Gun pocket top 1` from Account 1073383511, at 2,614 weekly favourites, buys Close Quarters, Monster Rounds, Swift Striker, Point Blank and Glass Cannon and never invests in spirit at all, which is a coherent answer to a hero whose abilities are all positioning.

## The build on the 2026-08-22 Update patch

Pocket was not named in this patch, and only one of its two lines reaches him at all: Restorative Locket dropped from 10% Spirit Resistance to 8% and appears in 13 of his 40 sampled builds, while Radiant Regeneration's cut from 70 to 65 heal on cast appears in 2. Thirteen lists in forty is a real number — check whether the locket is in your own build before you buy it out of habit.

None of the five published lists has been revised since the update. The three weekly sets — 3,431 favourites from Account 1462456789, 2,614 from Account 1073383511 and 2,032 from Account 272679564 — were last touched between 2026-04-14 and 2026-04-27, and both lifetime sets are older still: `Carney - Pocket Gaming` from Account 113256308 carries 157,393 favourites from 2024-08-01, and an 87,860-favourite set from Account 394955797 dates to 2024-09-18 and publishes a name this page does not reprint. Take shape from the recent three and nothing else.

## Abilities: what to level and when

Flying Cloak first on all three weekly lists, with Enchanter's Satchel second and Barrage third. That order is the hero admitting what his early game is: two movement abilities online before the damage one, because a Pocket who cannot leave a lane he is losing does not reach the half of the match where he wins.

The older lifetime list that records an order opens on Barrage instead, and the other records none at all. Barrage first is the lane-trade line, and it belongs to a version of the early game his own win rate under 30 minutes does not support.

Affliction goes last everywhere it is recorded. The ultimate cannot finish anyone, so it is pressure rather than a kill button, and points spent on the cloak buy more of the thing that actually gets you into range.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

The two RankLock lists disagree about who beats him, and the difference is instructive. His worst matchup rows are Victor at 41.1% over 203,456 matches, Seven at 41.4% over 260,880, Graves at 42.3% over 232,585, McGinnis at 43.2% over 124,985 and Kelvin at 43.8% over 111,170. The confirmed counter rows put Graves first at 39.6% over 24,111 games and Seven second at 40.6% over 21,205 — both lower than the wider table says — and then add a name the matchup five never mentions: Warden, at 42.6% over 22,641.

Read the overlap as one lesson and the outlier as another. Graves and Seven appear on both lists because they punish a channel that has to hold sight, so against them the fix is to open with the cloak from an angle rather than to start Barrage and hope. Warden appears only where the evidence is tightest, which makes him the lane to respect even though nothing in the broad table warns you about it.

His best rows sit barely above even: Venator at 52.0% over 261,689 matches, Sinclair at 52.0% over 105,898, Silver at 51.3% over 182,207, Bebop at 50.8% over 268,094 and Mina at 50.6% over 261,040. There is no lane here to bully, only lanes that let him farm undisturbed, which is all he needs.

## Items that go well

No ally clears RankLock's reporting floor with him, so there is no duo to draft around and no partner whose cooldowns yours are waiting on. Build him as a solo threat and pick your moments off the map rather than off a teammate's opener.

Weight the item slots by evidence. Ethereal Shift at 55.2% over 1,057,556 games and Diviner's Kevlar at 56.2% over 849,225 are the two rows with enough behind them to plan around, with Spiritual Overflow at 56.4% over 693,257 and Cheat Death at 56.9% over 601,832 close behind.

Leech at 54.3% over 182,512 games and Crippling Headshot at 53.7% over 175,842 are the honest middle of the table. Spellslinger reads 55.3% across 92,322, Mystic Reverb 54.6% across 55,085, Infuser 53.9% across 56,708 and Silencer 54.6% across 12,775 — the last of those on a table small enough that it should never decide a build.
