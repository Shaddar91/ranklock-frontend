## Who plays Mo & Krill, and why

Take Mo & Krill when your team has no way to reach the enemy doing the damage, and plan to be finished before the match gets long. He wins 56.0% of games decided under 30 minutes, 52.3% of those running 30 to 45 and 50.3% past 45, a slide of 5.7 points that turns every decision on him into a question about the clock. He is a 930 HP body travelling at 8.0 m/s on 3 stamina, so the reach is real; all that is left is choosing where to spend it.

Every button pays him for standing on top of someone. Scorn damages the ring around him and returns health for what it hits, far more when that is a hero. Burrow drops him under the floor, fast and armoured, and surfacing damages, slows and knocks up whoever was standing there. Sand Blast sprays a cone that disarms as well as hurts. Combo is a channelled hold-and-stun, and a kill inside the channel raises his maximum health for the rest of the match.

## How to play Mo & Krill as an Offensive initiator

Choose the target before you go under. Burrow commits you: you surface where you decided to surface and the knock-up on the exit is your opening, so the engagement is settled underground rather than improvised on arrival. Land beside the hero your team can finish, not the nearest body.

Then hold them with Combo and let everyone else spend it. The channel keeps one enemy still while five other people shoot, which makes the ultimate a purchase order for your team's damage rather than yours. Two buys mark out the players who understand that: Phantom Strike, which closes the last stretch of a gap, is bought 3.9 times more often on him than the roster average, and Torment Pulse — damage that ticks on whoever you are standing over — 4.1 times.

Keep Sand Blast for after the opener. A disarm is wasted on the hero already knocked into the air and worth a fight against the one still shooting you.

## How to play Mo & Krill as a Damage carry

Scorn is not a spell you cast in a fight, it is the reason you are still standing in one. It damages everything around him and heals him for the hits, with heroes paying far more than anything else, so the habit to build is holding it whenever an enemy is inside the ring and farming with it the rest of the time.

Buy the amplifiers with the largest tables behind them. Escalating Exposure wins 60.7% of 2,499,870 games on him and Scourge 58.9% of 2,928,652 — the two biggest samples the hero has, and both sell the same thing: more damage per second spent in the place he already has to be.

Spirit Burn belongs in that group at 60.6% across 958,120 games. Mystic Reverb is the tempting thin row at 59.2% over 56,986, which makes it a lead rather than a rule.

## How to play Mo & Krill as a Tank

Stand where the fight has to come to you, and let the ultimate pay you for the trouble. A kill during Combo adds maximum health permanently, so a Mo & Krill who lands two channels is a bigger body by the second objective than the 930 HP he started with.

Buy resistance ahead of healing. Colossus wins 59.2% of 457,961 games on him and Witchmail 58.6% of 1,835,458, the hero's second-largest table. Cheat Death reads 59.1% across 139,656 games and Divine Barrier 58.8% across 44,458, and the sample size is the whole difference between trusting those two rows.

Holding ground has to end something. His curve falls the longer a match runs, so ground taken and not converted into an objective is ground rented.

## Abilities: what to level and when

Scorn first, on all five published lists. That is as close to settled as this hero gets, and it follows from one ability doing damage, sustain and farm in the same cast.

The second point is where they part. Two weekly lists take Burrow, the third takes Combo, and both lifetime lists take Sand Blast. Burrow second is the aggressive lane read, because the exit knock-up threatens a kill before anyone owns resistances. Sand Blast second is the safe one: a disarm answers whoever is beating you in a straight trade.

Combo goes last on four of the five, and that is not a demotion. The ultimate needs teammates standing ready to use it, while a point in Scorn pays out on every wave whether anyone is watching or not.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Three heroes beat him, and both of RankLock's lists name the same three. He wins 44.4% of 252,175 matches against Seven, 45.8% of 249,571 against Victor and 46.7% of 255,552 against Graves; the counter rows agree at 46.5% over 29,451 against Victor, 46.7% over 25,974 against Seven and 47.1% over 29,227 against Graves. Kelvin at 46.9% over 117,323 and McGinnis at 47.8% over 123,065 complete the worst five.

Read all three as one problem rather than three. Each of them punishes the moment you are visible and standing still, which is exactly what Scorn asks you to be. Arrive underground instead of on foot, open with the exit knock-up, and save the disarm for whoever is still firing afterwards.

The lanes that go his way go his way by a margin. He wins 56.7% of 113,338 matches against Sinclair, 55.6% of 258,519 against Venator, 55.4% of 206,882 against Mina, 54.9% of 143,284 against Pocket and 54.1% of 195,326 against Silver — five rows where the hero is favoured before anyone presses a button, which is licence to take the fight rather than farm past it.

## Items that go well

Buy Escalating Exposure and Scourge before the table argues you anywhere else. They carry 2,499,870 and 2,928,652 of his games at 60.7% and 58.9%, and nothing else on the hero has that much weight behind it.

Witchmail at 58.6% over 1,835,458 and Spirit Burn at 60.6% over 958,120 come next, both on samples large enough to trust without an argument about who bought them.

The thin rows are worth a slot and not a plan: Mystic Reverb at 59.2% over 56,986 games, Divine Barrier at 58.8% over 44,458 and Magic Carpet at 58.5% over 216,782.

Three allies lift him, and all three do the same job. Vindicta wins 54.7% of the 20,494 games they share, Wraith 53.6% of 23,297 and Haze 52.8% of 23,973 — heroes who convert a target that cannot move, which is the only thing the channel produces.
