Pick the spirit build when you want Bashdown itself to be the event that ends the fight. Every Spirit item on this page feeds the slam: more spirit power on the hit, more healing off it, and a shorter wait until the next one. The page follows one published lifetime build, 21,144 favourites and updated 2026-08-29 for the 2026-08-22 Update patch, that passed the packet's spirit gate outright.

## What this build buys, tier by tier

Open with the items that make the slam pay for itself. Spirit Strike adds damage to Bashdown directly, Melee Lifesteal and Lifestrike heal you off the same hit, and Extra Charge banks a second slam early so you can spend one on the wave and keep one for the enemy. Close Quarters and Point Blank cover the brawling you still do between cooldowns, and Enchanter's Emblem plus Recharging Rush keep you healthy enough to stay in slam range.

Take Spirit Snatch as soon as your early core is done. The build's annotation explains the ordering that makes it core: your melee damage lands before the spirit damage, so the resistance steal applies to the slam that follows it. Compress Cooldown is the other early pickup, bought to get your main damage source back faster, in the author's own framing.

Cross the mid-game investment step into cooldown and scaling. Superior Cooldown and Rapid Recharge shorten the loop, Melee Charge keeps your swings threatening while the slam is down, and Tankbuster turns the burst onto high-health targets. The late slots go to Surge of Power, Crushing Fists and Focus Lens, with Warp Stone or Phantom Strike as the one mobility active you commit a key to. Boundless Spirit, Spirit Burn and Echo Shard are the build's listed flex options when the game goes long enough to afford them.

## Ability order for this build

Max Bashdown first, then Blasted, then Rising Ram, taking Chain Gang whenever points are offered. Bashdown leads because the whole build is a multiplier on it: each point raises the slam, and each spirit item raises it again. Blasted second because your melee still reloads the gun and marks victims as wrecked, which keeps you useful while the slam is on cooldown. Rising Ram third, since on this build the carry-into-the-air engage matters more as a delivery system for Bashdown than as damage itself. Chain Gang stays on its normal schedule: the chain that blocks movement abilities is how you hold a target inside slam range for the second hit.

## When to pick this over the standard build

Run it when the enemy team folds to burst and stacks health. Tankbuster and the spirit multipliers are at their best against high-health fronts, and a slammed backline cannot out-sustain a fight that starts at half its health bar.

Time your expectations to Billy's curve. His win rate moves from 44.0% in matches under 30 minutes to 48.5% in the 30-to-45-minute window and 48.2% past 45 minutes across RankLock's badge-grouped competitive matches, a hero who peaks in the mid-to-late game. The spirit build front-loads its damage into the slam window, so it suits games where your team fights on your cooldowns. Stay on the standard bruiser build when your team needs sustained gun damage over a long frontline grind, because this version spends its souls making one button bigger.

## Matchups this build changes

Accept the same hard matchups as any Billy build and change how you enter them. Graves holds him to a 40.0% win share over 31,507 games on the counter endpoint, with Victor at 42.4% over 35,704 games and Seven at 42.8% over 31,315 games; on the matchup endpoint those three sit at 43.6% over 416,851 matches, 44.4% over 452,096 matches and 43.2% over 432,702 matches respectively. Against them, hold Bashdown for the moment their key defensive ability is spent, because your whole pattern fails if the slam lands into a counter.

Spend the slam freely in the good matchups. Billy beats Sinclair at a 55.5% win rate over 177,445 matches, Venator at 54.2% across 452,224 matches, and Mina at 53.6% over 356,919 matches. Burst matters most against the heroes who want long trades, and these are the games where one fully stacked slam flips the lane.
