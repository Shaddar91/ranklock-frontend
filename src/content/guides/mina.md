## Who plays Mina, and why

Play Mina only if you are willing to give up the early game on purpose, because the whole hero is a bet on the clock. She wins 41.7% of matches decided under 30 minutes, 44.5% of those running 30 to 45 and 48.5% past 45 — a 6.8-point climb, and every line below is written to reach the far end of it. She is small and quick at 660 HP, 6.5 m/s and 2 stamina, and none of that survives a trade she did not choose.

The kit compounds if you let it. Rake is a cone of spirit damage that pays a bonus against missing health and heals her when it finishes a unit. Sanguine Retreat is a brief untargetable flight she can recast inside a short window. Love Bites adds spirit damage to her bullets and, with enough of them on one target, builds a bite. Nox Nostra releases a bat cloud that damages and silences, and every bite she has ever landed adds bats to it permanently.

## How to play Mina as a Damage carry

Shoot the same target for longer than feels comfortable. Love Bites builds per target and the ultimate keeps every bite you land, so spraying across a fight throws away the only mechanic on the hero that compounds. Pick a body, stack it, and let the bat count do the arithmetic.

Rake goes on the hurt one, never the healthy one. It hits harder against missing health and heals her when it finishes a unit, so it is a finisher and a sustain button at once; opening with it spends both halves for nothing.

Buy survival before damage, which is not the usual advice for a carry. Cheat Death wins 55.8% of 305,259 games on her, Ethereal Shift 54.6% of 1,548,492 and Diviner's Kevlar 54.8% of 179,059. On a hero with 660 HP whose payoff arrives late, the item that keeps you alive is the item that deals damage.

## How to play Mina as an Offensive initiator

Enter on Sanguine Retreat and keep the recast for the exit. The flight is brief, untargetable and recastable inside a short window, so the discipline is simple: never spend both halves getting in.

Nox Nostra is not an opener until the bites are banked. Early it is a silence with a thin cloud; late it is everything you stacked all match. Hold it for the fight that decides an objective rather than the skirmish that starts one.

Silencer at 54.2% over 64,784 games sharpens the same plan, and Spellslinger tops her table at 57.1% — but across only 26,563 games, her smallest sample, so treat that one as a lead rather than a rule.

## Abilities: what to level and when

There is nothing to decide here. All five published lists take Love Bites first, then Rake, then Sanguine Retreat, then Nox Nostra — five authors across two item eras and two languages, in complete agreement.

Play in that order as well. Love Bites first because it is the only ability that improves every bullet you fire and the only one that feeds the ultimate. Rake second because it is what turns a stacked target into a dead one. Sanguine Retreat third because its value is a decision rather than a rate, and points buy less of a decision.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Three heroes take Mina apart in RankLock's own matches, and the counter endpoint makes them worse rather than better. She wins 39.6% of 317,539 matches against Victor, 41.1% of 480,755 against Seven and 41.2% of 447,970 against Graves; the counter rows read Victor 38.2% over 27,647 games, Graves 38.6% over 40,467 and Seven 40.6% over 34,397. Kelvin at 41.8% over 161,161 matches and McGinnis at 42.0% over 188,966 are barely kinder.

There is no clever answer to those lanes. Take the safe farm and refuse everything else, because her best matchups are close to even anyway — Sinclair 51.3% over 169,038 matches, Venator 50.6% over 446,540, Bebop 50.1% over 424,645, Mirage 49.9% over 227,698 and Silver 49.8% over 317,508. The game is won by reaching the part of the clock where she scales, not by out-laning anyone in the first half of it.

## Items that go well

Buy what her own players already reach for. Mina players buy Spirit Rend 5.9 times more often than the roster average and Quicksilver Reload 3.6 times, which is a build philosophy in two numbers: keep the magazine turning so the bites keep landing.

After that her table is thin and worth reading honestly. Spiritual Overflow wins 54.1% of 1,661,867 games and Siphon Bullets 54.6% of 594,668, both on real samples; Mystic Reverb reads 55.2% over 95,421 and Crippling Headshot 53.7% over 332,838.

Do not plan around a duo. Every pairing RankLock reports on her is a losing one — 48.9% across 23,274 games with Mo & Krill, 43.4% across 20,666 with Billy and 42.8% across 22,942 with Bebop — so pick her because your team can protect a late scaler, not because of who else is on it.
