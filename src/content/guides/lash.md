## Who plays Lash, and why

Draft Lash when your team needs someone to start fights on the enemy's half of the map, and treat every fight as a route problem before it is a damage problem. He is fast and thin — 780 HP, 7.2 m/s, 3 stamina — and his win rate slides with the clock: 53.8% under 30 minutes, 52.2% from 30 to 45 and 49.4% past 45, a 4.4-point fall.

The kit is three movement answers and one payoff. Ground Strike is a stomp on the floor and a dive when he is already airborne. Grapple pulls him through the air to a target and resets his air jumps and dashes. Flog is a whip that heals him for a share of what it deals. Death Slam whips several enemies up, stuns them, and slams them back down.

## How to play Lash as an Offensive initiator

Dive on cooldowns, not on health bars. Health comes back, a spent escape does not, so the question before every slam is whether the target used their movement ability in the last six seconds. If they did, you land. If they did not, you are gambling — the same read RankLock's [dive timing post](/blog/lash-dive-timing/) teaches, and nothing in the current numbers moves that window.

Grapple is the setup, not the exit. It resets air jumps and dashes on contact, so the approach that works is a chain: gain height, Grapple something, arrive with a dash still in hand. Players who spend the reset getting in have nothing left to get out with.

Slam toward your own team. Death Slam decides where several bodies land, and dropping them next to your team rather than behind you is the entire difference between an opening and a solo death.

## How to play Lash as a Damage carry

Use Flog to stay, not to open. It heals a share of what it deals, so the trades you win in the air are what pay for the next dive; leading with it spends the sustain half on nothing.

Then buy the spirit burst that turns Death Slam from a stun into a kill. Mystic Reverb wins 60.7% of 2,171,837 games on him and Spirit Burn 58.1% of 4,706,190, the largest table on the hero by a wide margin. Spellslinger reads 62.0% over 258,709.

The gun half is not a fallback. Crippling Headshot wins 59.1% of 1,386,013 games and Weakening Headshot 57.8% of 1,130,957, and Lash players buy Headhunter 5.9 times more often than the roster average. A stunned pile is a stationary set of heads.

## How to play Lash as a Tank

Buy the ability to survive your own ultimate. Death Slam puts you inside the enemy team by design, so the armour rows in the published lists are not filler — they are what the button costs. Every set here carries a whole block of resistances rather than a token pair.

Restorative Locket is the one this patch touched on him. Its Spirit Resistance dropped from 10% to 8% and it sits in 16 of his 40 sampled builds, the heaviest exposure of anything the patch moved on this hero. Keep it anyway; it is still the cheapest way to survive the landing, and it is simply worth a little less than the lists assume.

Hold Unstoppable for the teams that cancel you. Two of the published lists annotate it for exactly that, and a Death Slam interrupted mid-cast is a dive you paid for and never took.

## Abilities: what to level and when

Flog first on three of the four lists that record an order; the fourth opens on Ground Strike. All four then put Grapple third and Death Slam last.

Read the split as a lane question. Flog first when you are trading, because the heal on the strike is what keeps you out there without walking home. Ground Strike first when you have the room to farm and want the clear instead.

Grapple third is deliberate. Its value is the reset, and a reset does not scale with points the way the two damage abilities do.

## Hard counters, and how to play around them

Three heroes beat Lash badly in RankLock's own matches, and the counter endpoint names the same three. He wins 44.8% of 451,692 matches against Seven, 44.9% of 312,524 against Victor and 45.9% of 393,343 against Graves; the counter rows read Victor 45.1% over 27,711 games, Graves 45.7% over 39,901 and Seven 46.2% over 39,262. McGinnis and Kelvin both sit at 46.5%, over 196,260 and 185,649 matches.

Against those five the dive is the trap rather than the answer, because each of them either holds ground you have to cross or still has the ability that answers your landing. Wait for the second cooldown, not the first. The lanes to press instead are Sinclair at 56.1% over 182,284 matches, Venator at 55.4% over 449,618, Bebop at 54.0% over 497,312, Silver at 53.9% over 281,195 and Mina at 53.8% over 424,961.

## Items that go well

Buy the arrival before the damage. Magic Carpet tops his table at 64.0% over 761,808 games, and Lash players buy Majestic Leap 6.2 times more often than the roster average — two answers to one problem, which is being above the fight when it starts.

Silencer at 60.4% over 191,980 games is what decides enemy ultimates, and Long Range at 59.7% over 520,810 keeps the whip live from further out. Lucky Shot reads 63.4% across only 37,429 games and Spiritual Overflow 57.1% across 60,655, so both are leads rather than rules.

Pick partners who convert the pile. Lash wins 57.8% of the 22,622 games he shares with Victor, 55.5% of 20,183 with Ivy and 53.4% of 22,424 with Wraith, and every one of those three finishes a stunned group faster than he can alone.
